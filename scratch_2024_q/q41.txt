In Octopus laqueus, two distinct sleep stages are observed: active (AS) and quiet (QS). Active sleep is
marked by active bouts, which include only specific body and eye movements along with transitions in
skin patterns, occurring between periods of quiet sleep. There are potential parallels between verte-
brates' non-REM and REM (Rapid Eye Movement) sleep phases and octopuses' QS and AS stages, respec-
tively. Researchers measured the occurrence of active bouts in the group of octopuses during the day
and night and in the interrupted conditions as seen in Figure 1.

Figure 1. A Average number of active bouts per hour over 36 hours before (Q) and after (R)
2-day sleep deprivation (darkness is depicted in gray background areas). B Probability of the
next bout (Y-axis) occurring N minutes after (X-axis) a normal (S) or interrupted (T) bout. C
Active bouts per hour (Y-axis) during 3 days of constant darkness (darkness is depicted in gray
background areas).
