<h2>Family</h2>
<div class="tribute-section-sub">The ground beneath everything</div>

<p>Biology olympiads are not a solo endeavor. Every hour spent studying at 2 AM, every weekend sacrificed for practice papers, every time I said "I can't come, I'm preparing". Someone else was absorbing the cost of that. My family carried the invisible weight of this journey without once asking me to put it down.</p>

<p>They did not always understand what IBO was, or why a fourteen-year-old needed to memorize the countercurrent multiplication mechanism in the nephron. But they understood that it mattered to me, and that was enough. The meals that appeared without me asking. The silence maintained when I was deep in a chapter. The quiet faith that this would lead somewhere, even when I could not promise it would.</p>

<p>Nothing I build, not BiOGuide, not any score, not any medal exists without them choosing, every single day, to let me pursue this. That is not a small thing. It is the foundation.</p>

<p> There is, though, one more person I'd like to mention here, not by name. They'll know. I want to thank them for i cant even write down what-not.</p><p> I know i am not an easy person to stay with (I think those of you who have read the notes and the writing styles will agree with me). I know that i am not even close to what they deserve. I know that I have messed up many times, and I know that I know that I have not been thoughtful enough way too many times for apologies to mean anything.</p>
<p>Yet, for some reason that only they will ever know, they still choose me somehow.</p>
<p>I want to thank them for never losing their hopes on me. I want to thank them for waking up every day and choosing me all over again, however hard it may be for them. Thank you for being there, always.</p>
</div>

<div class="tribute-section">
<h2>Teachers</h2>
<div class="tribute-section-sub">The ones who saw further</div>

<p>There is a specific kind of teacher who changes everything: the one who sees you aiming at something difficult and, instead of cautioning you to be realistic, hands you a harder problem. The ones who stayed after class, who answered the questions that were not on the syllabus, who pointed at a mechanism I had half-understood and said "no, look deeper."</p>

<p>For me these teachers were not those who taught me biology directly, in fact, i have rarely had any teachers for that.But those who i truly want to tribute, taught me how to think ; how to read a question properly, how to sit with confusion until it resolves, how to know the difference between understanding something and merely recognizing it. That difference is the entire gap between a good score and a great one.
I'd like to mention by name; Dr. Abhishek Chitnis, Dr. Kuldeep Bharadwaj, Mr. Yogesh Vyas, and Mrs. Mahak Sankhla.
Thank you for always believing in me.</p>

<p>I cannot list every teacher here without the list becoming incomplete in some way that matters. So instead: if you taught me something that made me better at this, and you're reading this page, you know who you are. Thank you for not giving up.</p>
</div>

<p>IBO-0 was not a notebook in the way most people think of notebooks. It was a year of preparation compressed into a single physical object, 360 pages of distilled IBO notes, hand-selected previous year problems with worked solutions, mechanism diagrams, mnemonic systems, edge-case facts that show up once every three years and cost you four marks when they do. I remember i used to write down actual links in the notes, something like https://imissibo-0/1274776363998/, all by hand. It was the single most useful resource i'd ever need.</p>

<p>It was the kind of thing you build once. You do not write a notebook like that twice, because the first time you are learning, and the second time you are just copying. The value of IBO-0 was not in the pages themselves. It was in the process of choosing what to include. Every page was a decision: is this worth the space? Will I need this under exam pressure? If I have thirty seconds to look something up, will this page save me?</p>

<p>By the time it was done, I could open it to almost any section and find exactly the note I needed. It was my entire preparation strategy in physical form.</p>

<div class="tribute-lost">
<div class="tribute-lost-label">Lost in transit</div>
IBO-0 was lost on one of the flights to IBO 2026. I remember keeping it in the magazine space of the seat in front of me, and never again. The kind of loss that is too mundane to be dramatic and too significant to shrug off. 
</div>
</div>

<p>I will not pretend there is a neat lesson in losing IBO-0. Sometimes you do everything right and the thing you built still disappears. Sometimes you do everything right, but in the end it doesn't work out. What I can say is this: IBO-0 might not have come to my use when i needed it the most, but the things that i learned while building it were the actual lessons worth learning. It taught me how to put so much work in and create something original. I will always be proud of this creation.</p>

<p>The notebook was the artifact. The preparation was the thing that mattered. And the preparation survived.</p>

<p>IBO-0 is also part of why BiOGuide exists the way it does. If your notes can be lost, your tracking should not be. The <a href="/dashboard/">Study Dashboard</a> is, in some sense, a digital version of the problem IBO-0 solved, keeping your knowledge organized, visible, and pointed at the gaps that matter most. It cannot be left on a plane.</p>
</div>
