Finland has sent students to the International Biology Olympiad ([IBO](/ibo-preparation/)) since 1997, and at the 37th IBO in Vilnius, Lithuania (12 to 19 July 2026) the four-student team came home with three bronze medals and one honourable mention.<sup>[3](#src-3)</sup> This page outlines how the path to that team works according to the sources we could read. It is a map of the structure, not an insider's account of what the exams feel like.

## The path at a glance

| Stage | What happens | How sure we are |
|---|---|---|
| Round 1, national test | A multiple choice and true or false test on a digital platform, taken at school in April | Good for the structure; the 2026 date is from the organiser's page |
| Training group and August week | The best of round 1 are invited to a five-day training week in August with a mock IBO exam | Medium; the IBO's page is self-reported and undated in places |
| Spring training and final | Four to five days of training the following April, then a final exam that selects the team | Medium; how the final places are decided is not stated |
| IBO team | Up to four students, selected through the competition and the Helsinki and Aalto Junior training | Good that the team is four; the exact selection rule is not stated |

## Stage 1: the national test

The first round is a national test on a digital platform, taken at school, with multiple choice and true or false questions.<sup><a href="#src-1">1</a></sup> The IBO's country page puts it in April, and the organiser's competition page lists 23 April 2026 as the date of the biology competition.<sup><a href="#src-1">1</a>,<a href="#src-2">2</a></sup> All upper secondary students can take part, and the IBO's page says students who are too old for the IBO may join for fun.<sup><a href="#src-1">1</a></sup> Participation figures are given by the IBO's page for 2019 to 2025 and are typically 550 to 700 students from 66 to 100 schools.<sup><a href="#src-1">1</a></sup> The organiser's 2026 pages are partly behind a password, so we could not read the current rules in full.

## Stage 2: the training group and the August week

The best performers in round 1 are invited to a training group, taking the IBO age rules into account.<sup><a href="#src-1">1</a></sup> The IBO's page describes a five-day training week in August at Aalto University, ending with a mock IBO exam, theory and practical, at the University of Helsinki.<sup><a href="#src-1">1</a></sup> A University of Helsinki report from 2022 describes the training as two hands-on weeks, one on molecular biology with Aalto Junior in the autumn and one on plant and animal biology and ecology in the spring with the Finnish Museum of Natural History (Luomus) and the Faculty of Biological and Environmental Sciences, alongside independent study and an online portal.<sup><a href="#src-4">4</a></sup> That report was written in Finnish, and our reading of it is a translation. The costs of the training weeks are covered, according to the IBO's page.<sup><a href="#src-1">1</a></sup>

## Stage 3: spring training and the final exam

According to the IBO's page, the following April there are four to five more days of training, followed by a final exam that selects the Olympic team.<sup><a href="#src-1">1</a></sup> We read the whole cycle as running from the April test of one year to the final in the April of the next, with the IBO in July, but the pages we could read do not set this out in one place. How many students are invited to the training group and how the final exam places are decided are listed in the box below.

## Stage 4: the IBO team

Up to four students represent Finland, and the University of Helsinki's 2026 news item says the 2026 team of four was selected through the national competition and the Helsinki and Aalto Junior training.<sup><a href="#src-3">3</a></sup> The same item names the Finnish National Agency for Education as a funder of the training and describes the 2026 result as the best among the Nordic countries.<sup><a href="#src-3">3</a></sup> The coordinator of the training is at the University of Helsinki's Luomus.<sup><a href="#src-3">3</a></sup> The IBO's page adds that the top ten of the final receive a diploma and a guaranteed place on a biotechnology and biomedicine programme at Tampere if they apply; check the current university policy before relying on this.<sup><a href="#src-1">1</a></sup> The next IBO is in Warsaw, Poland, in July 2027.<sup><a href="#src-3">3</a></sup>

### Points for an Author

If you went through this programme yourself, these are the points we most need you to settle. They are grouped here so that nothing uncertain is buried in the text above.

**Where the sources disagree**

- **When the training takes place.** The IBO's page puts the training group in August and the final stage the following April,<sup><a href="#src-1">1</a></sup> while the 2022 University of Helsinki report describes an autumn and a spring week without months.<sup><a href="#src-4">4</a></sup> These look compatible, but we could not confirm the exact months from an independent source.
- **Where the organiser's information is.** The IBO lists BMOL ry as the organiser and describes it as appointed at the Ministry of Education's request,<sup><a href="#src-1">1</a></sup> while the 2026 University of Helsinki item names the National Agency for Education as the funder and the University of Helsinki and Aalto as training partners.<sup><a href="#src-3">3</a></sup> These roles may simply differ, but no page we read sets them side by side.

**Gaps a firsthand author could fill**

- How many students are invited to the training group, and whether round 1 has a published cut-off score.
- How the final exam places are decided, and whether other results count.
- The exact months of the August and spring training weeks in the current year.
- The syllabus for round 1 and the final, and what the tests are like.
- Finland's results at the IBO in 2024 and 2025, which we could not confirm from a source we opened.
- The age limit used for the national training group.
- Whether the autumn and spring weeks are open to students outside the Helsinki and Espoo area, and who covers travel.

Our reading of the Finnish pages (the organiser's page and both University of Helsinki items) is a translation, and every date from them depends on it. ## BiOGuide tips for students aiming at this path

These are general tips from BiOGuide, not advice from a Finnish participant, and they apply to any student aiming at an IBO team.

- **Train for the international format early.** The IBO papers are in English and test reasoning on data and experiments, not only recall. The [IBO preparation guide](/ibo-preparation/) explains the format, and past IBO papers are in our [practice papers](/papers/).
- **Cover the training areas.** The Finnish training touches molecular biology, plants, animals and ecology, which line up with our notes on [cell and molecular biology](/resources/1-cell-molecular/), [plant physiology](/resources/5-plant-physiology/), [animal physiology](/resources/3-animal-physiology/) and [ecology](/resources/8-ecology/).
- **Practise the practical side.** The mock exam and the IBO both include practical exams, so learn the lab techniques and not only the theory.
- **Learn the English terms.** Even if you study in Finnish or Swedish, learn the English names of structures, processes and methods, because the IBO papers are written in English.
- **Use the official channels for anything dated.** Test dates and training weeks change every year. Your school's biology teacher and BMOL ry decide, not us.
