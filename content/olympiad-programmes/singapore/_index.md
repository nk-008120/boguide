Singapore sends a team of four to the International Biology Olympiad ([IBO](/ibo-preparation/)) and does very well. At the 37th IBO in Vilnius, Lithuania (12 to 19 July 2026), the Ministry of Education reported 3 gold and 1 silver medal and a joint fifth place among 78 countries and territories.<sup><a href="#src-3">3</a></sup> In 2025 in Quezon City, the Philippines, the team won 4 gold medals and was joint first.<sup><a href="#src-4">4</a></sup> This page outlines how the path to that team works according to the sources we could read. It is a map of the structure, not an insider's account of what the tests feel like.

## The path at a glance

| Stage | What happens | How sure we are |
|---|---|---|
| Theory test | An online multiple-choice test taken at school in November | Medium: from the IBO's undated page only |
| Practical tests | About the top 10% of theory scorers do four short practicals in one day | Medium: from the IBO's page only |
| Shortlist and training | 6 to 10 students are shortlisted; weekly discussions with university staff in April and May | Medium: from the IBO's page only |
| Residential camp | A five-day camp at the National Institute of Education in late May or early June | Medium: from the IBO's page only |
| IBO team | Four students, confirmed by the Ministry's results releases | Good for the team size and results; the selection of the four is not explained |

## Stage 1: the theory test

The IBO's page says schools nominate roughly their top 10% of Biology students, and between 250 and 350 students are invited each year. The test is 100 multiple-choice questions, sat online over three hours at the student's own school under teacher supervision in November, and weighted to the IBO syllabus.<sup><a href="#src-2">2</a></sup> Participation figures on the same page are 366 students from 22 schools in 2022, 382 from 22 in 2023 and 386 from 23 in 2024.<sup><a href="#src-2">2</a></sup> Schools are notified in October.<sup><a href="#src-2">2</a></sup> The test follows the IBO syllabus, and the page recommends Campbell's Biology but says no special resources are produced.<sup><a href="#src-2">2</a></sup>

## Stage 2: the practical tests

About the top 10% of theory scorers go on to practical tests: four one-hour practicals in a single day, taken in rotating groups of four students.<sup><a href="#src-2">2</a></sup> Medals and Certificates of Merit go to roughly the top 10% to 25%.<sup><a href="#src-2">2</a></sup> The Ministry of Education releases the results to the press after all the olympiads.<sup><a href="#src-2">2</a></sup>

## Stage 3: shortlisting, training and the camp

The IBO's page says about 6 to 10 students are shortlisted for Team Singapore training. Training has weekly discussions with academics from the National University of Singapore and the National Institute of Education, in April and May, followed by a five-day residential camp at the National Institute of Education in late May or early June that covers laboratory and dissection skills. Alumni have mentored the team since about 2005.<sup><a href="#src-2">2</a></sup> Shortlisted students are interviewed for potential and attitude, and staff assess practical skills, attitude and emotional intelligence at the camp.<sup><a href="#src-2">2</a></sup>

## Stage 4: the IBO team

Four students represent Singapore. The 2026 Ministry release credits medallists from NUS High School (three) and Raffles Institution (one), and names the delegation's team leaders from the National Institute of Education, SIBiol, A*STAR and the Ministry.<sup><a href="#src-3">3</a></sup> Results for the last three years are in the Ministry's press releases: 2024 in Astana, Kazakhstan, with 2 gold and 2 silver medals and fifth place among 74;<sup><a href="#src-5">5</a></sup> 2025 and 2026 are given above.

### Points for an Author

If you went through this programme yourself, these are the points we most need you to settle. They are grouped here so that nothing uncertain is buried in the text above.

**Where the sources disagree**

- **The format of the theory test.** The IBO's page describes one test of 100 questions over three hours.<sup><a href="#src-2">2</a></sup> A search summary we could not trace to its page described two tests of 40 questions each. We rely on the IBO's page, but the format may have changed between years.
- **The official website.** The IBO names the SIBiol activities page as the programme's site,<sup><a href="#src-2">2</a></sup> but the page returned no readable content when we tried to open it, so everything above rests on the IBO's page rather than on the organiser's own words.

**Gaps a firsthand author could fill**

- How the final four are chosen from the 6 to 10 shortlisted students. The sources we could read name interviews and camp assessment, but not the rule.
- The exact dates of each round and of the training, and what the IBO's page means by its November and April to May months in a given year.
- Whether schools have written criteria for nominating students, and how many students each school may send.
- The official syllabus document, if one exists beyond following the IBO syllabus.
- What the tests, the camp and the weekly sessions are actually like, and what students wish they had known.

## BiOGuide tips for students aiming at this path

These are general tips from BiOGuide, not advice from a Singaporean participant, and they apply to any student aiming at an IBO team.

- **Train for the international format early.** The IBO papers test reasoning on data and experiments, not only recall. The [IBO preparation guide](/ibo-preparation/) explains the format, and past IBO papers are in our [practice papers](/papers/).
- **Cover the whole IBO syllabus.** The national test follows it, so use our notes from [cell and molecular biology](/resources/1-cell-molecular/) to [evolution](/resources/15-evolution/).
- **Practise the practical side.** The selection includes four timed practical stations and a camp on laboratory and dissection skills, so learn techniques as well as theory. Our [practical notes](/resources/11-practical-1/) are a start.
- **Ask your school early.** Entry is through school nomination according to the IBO's page, so your Biology teacher is the first person to ask.
- **Use the official channels for anything dated.** Dates and quotas change every year. SIBiol and your school decide, not us.
