Australia sends four students to the International Biology Olympiad ([IBO](/ibo-preparation/)). At the 37th IBO in Vilnius, Lithuania (12 to 19 July 2026), the IBO's individual results list shows the four Australian students winning two silver and two bronze medals.<sup>[1](#src-1)</sup> We did not find a report from the national organiser to confirm that. This page outlines how the path to that team works according to the sources we could read. It is a map of the structure, not an insider's account of what the exam and the summer school feel like.

A note on the name. The national organiser does not call its programme a biology olympiad. It runs the Australian Science Olympiads (ASO), of which biology is one of four disciplines.<sup><a href="#src-3">3</a></sup> "Australian Biology Olympiad" is the title the IBO gives its page for Australia.<sup><a href="#src-2">2</a></sup>

## Stage 1: the ASO Biology Exam

The first stage is the Australian Science Olympiad Exam in Biology. It is a two hour online exam sat at school under supervision, with all students starting at the same time.<sup><a href="#src-3">3</a></sup> The IBO's page describes it as multiple choice.<sup><a href="#src-2">2</a></sup> In 2026 it was held on Tuesday 28 July.<sup><a href="#src-3">3</a></sup> The IBO's page places the exams in late July and early August, so the date moves a little from year to year.<sup><a href="#src-2">2</a></sup>

Only schools can register students, through the organiser's teacher portal. Individuals, tutoring centres, international schools and Australian citizens living overseas cannot enter. In 2026 the fee was 22 Australian dollars per student per exam, including GST.<sup><a href="#src-3">3</a></sup>

The official site says the exam suits students in Years 9 to 12, that a student must be at least 15 on 1 January of the summer school year, and that biology students must not have finished Year 12 by the time of the summer school.<sup><a href="#src-3">3</a></sup> The IBO's page words the year levels differently; see Points for an Author below.

Students receive a certificate showing how they performed. There is no fixed pass mark, and the official site suggests that scoring around 40 percent on past papers is a good position to be in.<sup><a href="#src-3">3</a></sup> The sources we could read do not name the award bands or say who writes the exam. The IBO's page puts biology entries at around 2,000, without a year.<sup><a href="#src-2">2</a></sup>

## Stage 2: the ASO Summer School

The top 24 students in each discipline are invited to the ASO Summer School at the Australian National University in Canberra in January.<sup><a href="#src-2">2</a>,<a href="#src-3">3</a></sup> Students there cover roughly a university-level subject and sit further exams.<sup><a href="#src-3">3</a></sup> A school news story about one 2026 team member mentions practical assessments as well.<sup><a href="#src-5">5</a></sup>

The official site says it lasts just over two weeks.<sup><a href="#src-3">3</a></sup> To be offered a place, a student must be an Australian citizen at the time of the offer and live in Australia. The 2026 fee was 3,500 Australian dollars, with financial help available.<sup><a href="#src-3">3</a></sup>

## Stage 3: team selection and training

Four students are chosen from the 24.<sup><a href="#src-2">2</a>,<a href="#src-3">3</a></sup> The sources we could read do not publish how: which exams count, and with what weight, is not stated.

The official site says the biology team has further training in April, without giving the place or the length.<sup><a href="#src-3">3</a></sup> Team members must be Australian citizens holding an Australian passport and must not have started university. Taking part in the international olympiad carried a fee of 3,500 Australian dollars in 2026, again with financial help available.<sup><a href="#src-3">3</a></sup>

## Stage 4: the IBO team

The teams for all the science olympiads are announced together at Parliament House in Canberra in June; in 2026 that was on 22 June.<sup><a href="#src-4">4</a>,<a href="#src-6">6</a></sup> From the exam to the IBO the whole process takes about twelve months, so the students who competed in July 2026 had sat the ASO exam in the middle of 2025.<sup><a href="#src-3">3</a>,<a href="#src-4">4</a></sup>

### Points for an Author

If you went through this programme yourself, these are the points we most need you to settle. They are grouped here so that nothing uncertain is buried in the text above.

**Where the sources disagree**

- **The name.** The organiser calls its programme the Australian Science Olympiads;<sup><a href="#src-3">3</a></sup> "Australian Biology Olympiad" is the IBO's title for its page.<sup><a href="#src-2">2</a></sup>
- **Which year levels can enter.** The official site says Years 9 to 12, provided biology students have not finished Year 12 by the summer school;<sup><a href="#src-3">3</a></sup> the IBO's page recommends Years 10 and 11 and says Year 12 students can sit the exam but cannot go further.<sup><a href="#src-2">2</a></sup> The official site is the more recent.
- **How long the summer school lasts.** 11 days on the IBO's page,<sup><a href="#src-2">2</a></sup> just over two weeks on the official site.<sup><a href="#src-3">3</a></sup>
- **How the four are chosen.** The organiser publishes no method. A third-party site, seen only in search results, describes a practical exam at the summer school and a three hour theory exam some weeks later; we could not check that and do not rely on it.

**Gaps a firsthand author could fill**

- How the four are chosen from the 24: which exams count and how much.
- Where the April training is held and how long it lasts.
- The names of the award bands on the exam certificate, and who writes the exam.
- How many students sat the biology exam in 2025 or 2026.
- What the syllabus contains. The organiser publishes a biology syllabus, but we did not open it.
- What the summer school is actually like, how students prepare for the exam alongside school, and what they wish they had known.

## BiOGuide tips for students aiming at this path

These are general tips from BiOGuide, not advice from an Australian participant, and they apply to any student aiming at an IBO team.

- **Start from the organiser's own materials.** The official site offers a biology syllabus, free past papers with answers, and an online question bank.<sup><a href="#src-3">3</a></sup> Read the syllabus first so you know what the exam can ask.
- **Do past papers under exam conditions.** Two hours online in one sitting is the real format. The organiser's remark about 40 percent tells you the papers are meant to be hard, so do not be discouraged by low scores early on.
- **Study beyond your school course.** Our notes on [cell and molecular biology](/resources/1-cell-molecular/), [animal physiology](/resources/3-animal-physiology/), [plant physiology](/resources/5-plant-physiology/), [genetics](/resources/7-genetics/) and [ecology](/resources/8-ecology/) cover the areas olympiad exams draw on.
- **Practise reading data.** Past IBO papers in our [practice papers](/papers/) are built around experiments and data, and the [IBO preparation guide](/ibo-preparation/) explains the international format.
- **Talk to your teacher early.** Only schools can register, and the organiser says it does not run exam training itself, so any preparation programme at your school is the school's own.<sup><a href="#src-2">2</a></sup>
- **Use the official channels for anything dated.** Exam dates, fees and eligibility rules change every year. The organiser decides, not us.
