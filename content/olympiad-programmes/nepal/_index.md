Nepal runs a national biology olympiad that the International Biology Olympiad ([IBO](/ibo-preparation/)) lists among its national olympiads, established in 2017.<sup><a href="#src-1">1</a></sup> The IBO's 2026 individual results from Vilnius, Lithuania list four competitors from Nepal.<sup><a href="#src-2">2</a></sup> Nepal's IBO history is still short and thinly documented, so this is a short outline with many open points. It is a map of the structure, not an insider's account of what the exams feel like.

## The path at a glance

| Stage | What happens | How sure we are |
|---|---|---|
| Round 1, local level | Multiple choice, a basic selection | Medium: from the undated IBO page |
| Round 2, district level | Multiple choice, a basic selection | Medium |
| Round 3, province level | Multiple choice and subjective questions, the first national selection | Medium |
| Online study and national camp | Training, sometimes online and sometimes in person, about 15 days | Medium |
| Round 4, national level, and practical round | Multiple choice and subjective questions, then a practical | Medium |
| IBO team | Not described in the sources we read | Low |

## The national rounds

The IBO's page for Nepal lists these steps in order: a local round and a district round, both multiple choice, then a province round with multiple choice and subjective questions, then online study and a national camp, then a national round and a practical round.<sup><a href="#src-1">1</a></sup> It says the organising structure has one central, seven province, 77 district and 753 local committees, and that training lasts 15 days and may be online or in person.<sup><a href="#src-1">1</a></sup> The page gives no quotas, dates or exam format beyond this, and the school year it describes runs from mid-April to mid-March.<sup><a href="#src-1">1</a></sup>

## Taking part in the IBO

At the 37th IBO in Vilnius (12 to 19 July 2026), the individual results list four competitors from Nepal.<sup><a href="#src-2">2</a></sup> We did not find a Nepal entry in the IBO 2025 ranking from Quezon City.<sup><a href="#src-3">3</a></sup> A 2023 news report says two shortlisted Nepali students could not travel to the IBO that year for lack of funds, and says a Nepali student won a bronze medal at the IBO in 2020; that report covers Nepal's science olympiads in general and not biology alone.<sup><a href="#src-4">4</a></sup> The sources we could read do not say how the team is chosen from the national rounds.

### Points for an Author

If you went through this programme yourself, these are the points we most need you to settle. They are grouped here so that nothing uncertain is buried in the text above.

**Where the sources disagree**

- **IBO membership status.** The IBO lists Nepal among its national olympiads with no stated status,<sup><a href="#src-1">1</a></sup> while Wikipedia, in a snapshot dated 2 June 2026, counts Nepal as a pending member.<sup><a href="#src-5">5</a></sup> Nepali competitors appear in the 2026 IBO results,<sup><a href="#src-2">2</a></sup> so we cannot tell what "pending" means in practice.
- **Who hosts Nepal's IBO entry.** The IBO names STAN as host,<sup><a href="#src-1">1</a></sup> while the 2023 news report names STEM Foundation Nepal as the national host for ten international olympiads and says STAN co-manages only the junior science olympiad.<sup><a href="#src-4">4</a></sup>
- **Participation figures.** The IBO page gives 1,200 students from 425 schools for 2025 and 640 from 320 schools for 2023 in its yearly list, but its text also mentions about 2,500 first round students.<sup><a href="#src-1">1</a></sup>

**Gaps a firsthand author could fill**

- How the IBO team is chosen and how many students it has each year.
- The dates, quotas and syllabus for each round, and what the exam language is.
- How a student registers at the local level, and whether there is a fee.
- What the rounds, the camp and the practical are actually like, and what students wish they had known.

## BiOGuide tips for students aiming at this path

These are general tips from BiOGuide, not advice from a Nepali participant, and they apply to any student aiming at an IBO team.

- **Train for the international format early.** The IBO papers are in English and test reasoning on data and experiments, not only recall. The [IBO preparation guide](/ibo-preparation/) explains the format, and past IBO papers are in our [practice papers](/papers/).
- **Practise the practical side.** The national path has a practical round and the IBO has practical stations, so learn lab techniques as well as theory. Our notes on [practical skills](/resources/11-practical-1/) are a start.
- **Build the core theory.** Work through [cell and molecular biology](/resources/1-cell-molecular/), [genetics](/resources/7-genetics/) and [plant physiology](/resources/5-plant-physiology/).
- **Learn the English terms.** Even if you study in Nepali, learn the English names of structures, processes and methods, because the IBO papers are written in English.
- **Use the official channels for anything dated.** Dates, eligibility and team selection change every year. The organisers decide, not us.
