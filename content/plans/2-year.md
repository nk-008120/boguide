# 2-Year Biology Olympiad Study Plan

## Who this plan is for

Anyone from complete beginner to school-biology level who has genuinely committed two years and can put in five to eight hours a week consistently. This is the runway for building real depth -- not just coverage, but the kind of understanding where you can answer questions you've never seen before because you know the mechanisms, not just the facts.

## What you can realistically achieve

Full mastery of the IBO syllabus, competitive standing at INBO/USABO level, and a fair shot at IBO qualification if your national system supports it. Two years gives you something shorter plans can't: the time to re-study topics after your first pass, which is where real mastery happens.

## Phase 1: Foundations (months 1-7)

These are the highest-frequency exam topics. Get them solid before moving on.

| Months | Section | What to focus on | Primary resource |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1-3 | [Cell & Molecular Biology and Biochemistry](/resources/1-cell-molecular/) | Cell structure, membrane transport, enzyme kinetics, metabolic pathways (glycolysis, Krebs, ox-phos, photosynthesis), signal transduction | Campbell Biology (bridge); Alberts Ch. 1-15 when Campbell isn't deep enough |
| 4-5 | [Genetics](/resources/7-genetics/) | Mendelian inheritance with extensions, linkage mapping, gene regulation (lac/trp operons, eukaryotic), molecular techniques | Campbell; Griffiths for linkage problems |
| 6-7 | Start [Animal Physiology](/resources/3-animal-physiology/) | Cardiovascular, respiratory, nervous system basics | Campbell; begin Silverthorn |

**Weekly rhythm:** 5-6 hours. Three sessions: two concept-learning (1.5 hrs each), one problem-solving (1.5 hrs). The problem-solving session should use end-of-chapter questions from your textbook, not just re-reading notes.

**How to know you're ready to move on:** After finishing a section, do a self-quiz from memory -- list the key mechanisms, draw the major pathways, explain the cause-and-effect chains. If you can do this without looking at notes, you're solid. If not, spend another week on the gaps before moving forward.

## Phase 2: Core systems (months 8-14)

| Months | Section | What to focus on | Primary resource |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8-9 | Finish [Animal Physiology](/resources/3-animal-physiology/) + [Animal Anatomy](/resources/2-animal-anatomy/) | Renal physiology, endocrine, immune system, comparative anatomy across phyla | Silverthorn; Hickman for comparative anatomy |
| 10-11 | [Plant Anatomy](/resources/6-plant-anatomy/) + [Plant Physiology](/resources/5-plant-physiology/) | Root/stem/leaf anatomy, photosynthesis at PS I/II level, C3/C4/CAM, phytohormones, water potential | Campbell; Taiz for mechanism-level plant physiology |
| 12-14 | [Ecology](/resources/8-ecology/), [Ethology](/resources/9-ethology/), [Evolution](/resources/15-evolution/) | Population dynamics, community ecology, behavioral ecology, population genetics (Hardy-Weinberg), speciation | Campbell; dedicated ecology text if needed |

**Weekly rhythm:** 6-8 hours. You're covering more sections now, so add a fourth session on weekends for broader revision of Phase 1 material. Don't let Cell Biology and Genetics decay while you study physiology.

**Bring in past papers at month 8.** From this point, do one past-paper problem set every two to three weeks. You won't score well yet -- that's fine. The purpose is diagnostic: which topics are you weaker on than you thought? Adjust your study priorities accordingly.

## Phase 3: Specialized topics (months 15-18)

| Months | Section | What to focus on |
|---|---|---|
| 15-16 | [Bioinformatics](/resources/10-bioinformatics/) | BLAST, sequence alignment, phylogenetic tree construction and interpretation |
| 17-18 | Practical sections ([11](/resources/11-practical-1/), [12](/resources/12-practical-2/), [13](/resources/13-practical-3/), [14](/resources/14-practical-4/)) | Microscopy, data analysis, experimental design, lab techniques (conceptual) |

**Bioinformatics** can be practiced entirely at home -- try the [IBO 2022 bioinformatics simulator](/ibo2022-sim.html) and work through online databases. Practical sections need lab access if possible; see the [IBO guide's practical preparation section](/ibo-preparation/#practical-preparation) for what you can and can't prepare without a lab.

## Phase 4: Revision and intensive practice (months 19-24)

This is where two-year plans pay off. You've covered everything; now you go deeper.

| Months | Focus |
|---|---|
| 19-20 | Full past papers weekly. Comprehensive error analysis after each paper -- for every wrong answer, identify: was it a knowledge gap, a reasoning error, or a reading error? |
| 21-22 | Targeted re-study of persistent weak areas. Go back to subject-specific textbooks (Alberts, Lehninger, Silverthorn) for topics that keep costing you marks. |
| 23-24 | Exam simulation: full papers under timed conditions, twice per week. Review between papers. Focus on speed and accuracy together. |

**Use the [Study Dashboard](/dashboard/)** if you've been submitting attempts to the leaderboard -- it tracks your mastery across all topics and identifies persistent weak areas automatically.

## Progress checkpoints

| Month | Checkpoint | Target |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | First past paper (diagnostic) | Any score -- this is for calibration, not assessment |
| 8 | Full paper mock | 35-45% (you haven't covered all topics yet) |
| 14 | Full paper mock | 55-65% |
| 18 | Full paper mock | 65-75% |
| 22 | Full paper mock | 75%+ |
| 24 | Pre-exam mock | 80%+ on topics in your target olympiad |

## What to skip

Nothing. If you find yourself wanting to skip sections, your timeline is longer than two years allows, and this plan isn't the right fit. Two years is a luxury -- use it to build genuine depth everywhere.
