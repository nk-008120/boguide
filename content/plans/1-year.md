## Who this plan is for

Students who already have school-biology foundations (AP Biology, CBSE/ICSE Class 12, or equivalent) and can put in eight to ten hours a week for a full year. This is the default plan for serious olympiad prep -- enough time to cover everything without cutting corners, but not enough time to waste on topics you already understand.

## What you can realistically achieve

Full syllabus coverage plus solid past-paper competence. Strong INBO/USABO showing likely. IBO qualification depends on your system's pathway and your peers, but you'll be well-prepared for whatever selection process comes next.

## Phase 1: Foundations (months 1-3)

| Month | Section | What to focus on | Weekly hours |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | [Cell & Molecular Biology and Biochemistry](/resources/1-cell-molecular/) | Enzyme kinetics, metabolic pathways, membrane transport, signal transduction. You likely know the basics from school -- push to mechanism-level understanding. | 8-10 |
| 2 | [Genetics](/resources/7-genetics/) | Mendelian inheritance with extensions, then molecular genetics (gene regulation, molecular techniques). Practice linkage problems -- they require a different skill than reading about genetics. | 8-10 |
| 3 | [Animal Physiology](/resources/3-animal-physiology/) (first half) | Cardiovascular, respiratory, nervous system. Start Silverthorn if Campbell's depth isn't enough for practice questions. | 8-10 |

**Primary resources:** Campbell Biology as the baseline. When Campbell's explanation of enzyme kinetics or signal transduction leaves you unable to answer IBO-level questions, pick up Alberts or Lehninger for those specific chapters.

**First diagnostic past paper at the end of month 2.** Don't wait until you've covered everything. Score doesn't matter -- the point is to learn what IBO questions actually look like and calibrate your expectations.

## Phase 2: Broaden (months 4-7)

| Month | Section | What to focus on |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | Finish [Animal Physiology](/resources/3-animal-physiology/) + [Animal Anatomy](/resources/2-animal-anatomy/) | Renal, endocrine, immune system. Comparative anatomy across major phyla. |
| 5 | [Plant Physiology](/resources/5-plant-physiology/) + [Plant Anatomy](/resources/6-plant-anatomy/) | Photosynthesis (PS I/II, C3/C4/CAM), phytohormones, water transport, root/stem/leaf anatomy. Don't skip this -- plant biology is 25-30% of IBO. |
| 6 | [Ecology](/resources/8-ecology/) + [Evolution](/resources/15-evolution/) | Population dynamics, community ecology, population genetics (Hardy-Weinberg quantitative problems), speciation. |
| 7 | [Ethology](/resources/9-ethology/) + [Bioinformatics](/resources/10-bioinformatics/) | Behavioral ecology, mating systems. BLAST basics, sequence alignment, phylogenetic tree reading. |

**Weekly rhythm:** 8-10 hours. Three concept sessions (2 hrs each) plus one longer weekend session (2-3 hrs) for past-paper practice or revision.

**Past papers from month 4 onward:** One paper every two weeks. After each paper, track which topics cost you marks. This feeds your revision priorities in Phase 3.

**Textbook upgrades during this phase:** Add subject-specific references for your weakest areas:
- Scoring below 50% on animal physiology questions? Add Silverthorn.
- Plant physiology questions feel alien? Add Taiz for the relevant chapters.
- Linkage problems consistently wrong? Add Griffiths' genetics chapter on mapping.

## Phase 3: Practice and refine (months 8-12)

| Months | Focus | Cadence |
|---|---|---|
| 8-9 | Practical sections and gap-filling | One past paper per week plus targeted revision on persistent weak areas |
| 10-11 | Intensive paper practice | Two papers per week. Full timed conditions. Detailed error analysis after each. |
| 12 | Exam simulation | Full papers every 2-3 days. Review between papers. Focus on time management and accuracy under pressure. |

**Error analysis protocol:** For every wrong answer, identify the type:

| Error type | What it means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Knowledge gap | You didn't know the fact or mechanism | Go back to the relevant textbook section and re-study |
| Reasoning error | You knew the fact but applied it wrong | Practice more questions on that topic -- the knowledge is there but not fluent |
| Reading error | You misread the question or missed a qualifier | Practice reading questions more carefully; slow down on "not" and "except" questions |

The [Study Dashboard](/dashboard/) automates much of this tracking if you submit your attempts to the leaderboard.

## Progress checkpoints

| Month | Checkpoint | Target |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | First diagnostic paper | Any score (calibration only) |
| 5 | Full paper mock | 45-55% |
| 8 | Full paper mock | 60-70% |
| 11 | Full paper mock | 70-80% |
| 12 | Pre-exam mock | 75%+ |

## What to skip

Move on from any subtopic once you get it, even if you feel you could go deeper. A year isn't enough for research-level depth on every subtopic. The goal is solid understanding of mechanisms across the full syllabus, not exhaustive mastery of any one area.
