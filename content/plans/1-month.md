# 1-Month Biology Olympiad Study Plan

## Who this plan is for

Students with solid foundations who need a structured final ramp-up. Not a plan for anyone trying to learn olympiad biology from scratch. If you've never opened Campbell, this timeframe won't get you there; be honest with yourself and set realistic goals.

## What you can realistically achieve

Paper-level fluency on topics you already partially know, sharper exam technique, and a clearer sense of your weak spots going into the exam.

## What to prioritize

Past papers as the primary learning tool. Every mistake becomes a targeted mini-revision on the specific concept you missed.

- **Week 1**: Diagnostic past paper. Analyze which sections cost you the most marks. Those are your revision priorities for weeks two and three.
- **Weeks 2-3**: Alternate days: focused revision on a weak section one day, timed past-paper practice the next. Every past-paper mistake goes into an errors journal.
- **Week 4**: Full-paper mocks every other day, spaced with error review and short recall sessions. Slow down on new content.

## What to skip

Any topic you don't already have a decent grasp of. You will not build lasting understanding of unfamiliar material in a month while also drilling papers. Accept the marks you'll lose there and focus on shoring up what you almost know.

## Daily rhythm

Fifteen to twenty hours a week, roughly two to three focused hours a day. Weekend blocks of four to five hours for full paper attempts. Sleep matters more than one more hour of study this close to the exam.

## When to bring in past papers

From day one. This whole plan is built around them.

## How to measure progress

Track your mock scores per section in a spreadsheet or notes app. The trend line matters more than any single score. If a section keeps costing you the same marks, that's what your revision should focus on this week.

## Resources

You shouldn't be starting new books at this stage. Use notes, [BiOrchive](/papers/) past papers, and whichever books you've already been using as reference for lookups only. Adding new resources this late usually hurts more than it helps.
