## Who this plan is for

Students with solid foundations who need a structured final ramp-up. You've already studied the core syllabus -- cell biology, genetics, physiology -- and you've done at least a few practice papers. If you've never opened Campbell Biology, this timeframe won't get you there. Be honest with yourself and set realistic goals.

This plan is also the right fit for students who qualified for INBO from NSEB, or for USABO Semifinal from the Open, and have 4-6 weeks before the next stage.

## What you can realistically achieve

Paper-level fluency on topics you already partially know, sharper exam technique, and a clearer map of your strengths and weaknesses going into the exam. You won't learn new material in depth -- but you can significantly improve your performance on material you already understand by practicing retrieval under timed conditions.

## Week-by-week breakdown

### Week 1: Diagnostic and priority map

**Day 1-2: Full diagnostic paper.** Take a complete past paper ([BiOrchive](/papers/)) under timed exam conditions. No notes, no breaks, strict timing.

**Day 3: Error analysis.** Go through every wrong answer and categorize:

| Category | Count | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| Knowledge gap (didn't know the fact/mechanism) | ___ | These topics need focused revision |
| Reasoning error (knew the fact, applied it wrong) | ___ | These topics need more practice problems, not more reading |
| Reading error (misread the question) | ___ | Slow down on "not," "except," "which of the following is false" questions |
| Time pressure (didn't reach the question) | ___ | Practice pacing strategy |

**Day 4-7: Priority revision.** Rank your knowledge gaps by frequency across the paper. The top 3-4 topics that cost you the most marks are your revision priorities for weeks 2 and 3. Go back to your textbook for these specific sections -- read, then immediately test yourself from memory.

### Week 2: Focused revision + paper practice

Alternate days:

| Day type | Activity | Duration |
|---|---|---|
| Revision day | Deep study on your #1 or #2 weak topic. Read the relevant textbook chapter, then test yourself with [Question Bank](/papers/question-bank/) questions on that topic. | 2-3 hrs |
| Paper day | Timed past paper. Afterward, quick error check -- are you still losing marks on the same topics, or have they improved? | 2-3 hrs |

**Target: 3 revision days + 2 paper days this week.** After each paper, update your error tracking. If a topic improves, move on to the next priority. If it doesn't, spend another revision day on it.

### Week 3: Intensify paper practice

**4-5 papers this week.** One every other day at minimum.

By now, your revision priorities should be sharpening. The topics that cost you marks in week 1 should be improving. New weak spots may have emerged -- address them in your revision sessions between papers.

**Between papers:**
- Review errors (30-45 min max -- don't re-read the whole paper)
- Targeted review of any topic that cost you 3+ marks on the paper you just did
- No new textbook chapters -- this is refinement, not new learning

**Time management focus:** Are you finishing papers with time to spare, or running out? If you're running out:
- Practice skipping questions you're unsure about and marking them for return
- Set a per-question time budget (e.g., 90 seconds per statement on IBO theory)
- Practice the "two-pass" strategy: answer everything you're confident about first, then go back to uncertain questions

### Week 4: Exam simulation and rest

**Days 1-3:** Two full mocks under strict exam conditions. Time it exactly. No notes. Simulate the real experience as closely as possible.

**Days 4-5:** Light review of any remaining weak spots from the last two mocks. Keep it to 1-2 hours per day maximum.

**Days 6-7:** Rest. Seriously. Sleep well. Don't cram. The evidence consistently shows that sleep consolidation matters more than last-minute review at this stage.

## What NOT to do in the last month

**Don't start a new textbook.** If you haven't read Alberts by now, starting it this week won't help. Use the references you already have.

**Don't try to learn a completely new topic from scratch.** If you've never studied bioinformatics, spending a week on it now means less time on topics you actually know and can score on. Accept the marks you'll lose on unfamiliar topics and maximize your score on familiar ones.

**Don't do papers without reviewing them.** A paper you don't analyze is wasted practice. The error analysis is where learning happens, not the attempt itself.

**Don't study 8+ hours a day.** Diminishing returns are real. 3-4 focused hours per day with good sleep beats 8 exhausted hours.

## Daily rhythm

Fifteen to twenty hours a week, roughly two to three focused hours a day. Weekend blocks of four to five hours for full paper attempts.

| Component | Hours/week |
|---|---|
| Past-paper attempts (timed) | 6-8 |
| Error analysis and review | 3-4 |
| Targeted topic revision | 4-6 |
| Rest and unfocused review | 2-3 |

## Resources

You shouldn't be adding new resources at this stage. Use:

- [BiOrchive](/papers/) for past papers (the core of this plan)
- [Question Bank](/papers/question-bank/) for drilling specific weak topics
- [Study Dashboard](/dashboard/) for tracking your mastery trends across attempts
- Whatever textbooks you've already been using -- as reference for lookups only
- Your own notes from earlier study

Adding new resources this late usually hurts more than it helps -- it fragments your attention and creates the illusion of progress without actual retrieval practice.
