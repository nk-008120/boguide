# 3-Month Biology Olympiad Study Plan

## Who this plan is for

Students with existing foundations targeting a specific exam, willing to put in twelve to fifteen hours a week. This plan makes explicit trade-offs; if you want full coverage, you need the [6-month plan](/plans/6-months/) or longer.

## What you can realistically achieve

Solid competence on the eight to ten highest-frequency exam topics, plus past-paper fluency. Rarer subtopics get deprioritized honestly; you'll lose some marks on them.

## What to prioritize

- **Month 1**: [Cell & Molecular Biology, Biochemistry](/resources/1-cell-molecular/) and [Genetics](/resources/7-genetics/). These two sections carry the largest weight across all three olympiads.
- **Month 2**: Human [Physiology](/resources/3-animal-physiology/), core [Plant Physiology](/resources/5-plant-physiology/), and basic [Ecology](/resources/8-ecology/).
- **Month 3**: Past papers, error analysis, and targeted revision.

## What to deprioritize

Comparative invertebrate anatomy detail, detailed ethology beyond core concepts, systematics and cladistics detail, and deep bioinformatics beyond BLAST basics. You should know these topics exist and recognize question types, but don't spend weeks mastering them.

## Weekly rhythm

Twelve to fifteen hours a week. Two focused study blocks on weekdays and one long weekend session for past papers. Consistency matters more than any single heroic session.

## When to bring in past papers

From week four onward. One per week in month two, two per week in month three.

## How to measure progress

Weekly quiz on the current focus topic. Full-paper mock every second weekend of month two. In month three, mocks become the primary learning tool, not just an assessment.

## Resources

Skip cover-to-cover reading. Use Grade S books as reference for the specific chapters covering your active topic. The [book database on Study Resources](/resources/) filters by category so you can find the right chapter without wading through indexes.
