# 6-Month Biology Olympiad Study Plan

## Who this plan is for

Students who have already worked through school biology (AP Biology, CBSE/ICSE Class 12, or equivalent) and can commit ten to twelve hours a week. You know what mitosis is, you've heard of the Krebs cycle, and you can draw a basic pedigree. If you're starting from scratch, this timeframe isn't realistic -- pick the [1-year plan](/plans/1-year/) or acknowledge you'll be triaging in the [3-month plan](/plans/3-months/).

## What you can realistically achieve

Coverage of the full syllabus at moderate depth, plus enough past-paper practice for functional exam readiness. You'll be competitive at NSEB/USABO Open level and solidly prepared for INBO/USABO Semifinal. Not enough time for research-level depth on niche subtopics -- that's a trade-off this plan makes honestly.

## Month-by-month breakdown

### Month 1: Cell Biology, Biochemistry, and Genetics

This is the most loaded month because these topics are the highest-frequency across all olympiads and everything else builds on them.

**Week 1-2: Cell & Molecular Biology**
- Cell structure and organelles (review quickly if you know this from school)
- Membrane transport -- go beyond osmosis: learn channel vs carrier proteins, primary vs secondary active transport, electrochemical gradients
- Enzyme kinetics: Michaelis-Menten, Km and Vmax, competitive vs non-competitive inhibition. This is tested quantitatively at INBO/IBO level.
- Signal transduction: G-protein coupled receptors, receptor tyrosine kinases, second messengers (cAMP, IP3/DAG, Ca2+)

**Week 3-4: Genetics**
- Mendelian inheritance with extensions (epistasis, pleiotropy, incomplete penetrance)
- Linkage analysis and chromosomal mapping -- practice problems, not just reading
- Gene regulation: lac operon, trp operon, eukaryotic transcription factors
- Molecular techniques: PCR, gel electrophoresis, restriction enzymes, CRISPR
- Pedigree analysis with complex patterns

**Resources:** Campbell Biology for both. Supplement with Lehninger Ch. 6 for enzyme kinetics calculations and Griffiths for linkage problem practice.

### Month 2: Animal Physiology and Anatomy

**Week 5-6: Core physiology**
- Cardiovascular system: cardiac cycle, action potentials in cardiac cells, blood pressure regulation
- Renal physiology: nephron structure, countercurrent multiplication, ADH and aldosterone regulation
- Nervous system: action potentials, synaptic transmission, sensory transduction

**Week 7-8: Extended physiology + anatomy**
- Endocrine system: hormone-receptor mechanisms, hypothalamic-pituitary axes, feedback loops
- Immune system: innate vs adaptive, T-cell activation, B-cell activation, antibody structure
- Respiratory system: gas exchange, hemoglobin cooperativity, Bohr effect
- Comparative anatomy: key differences across major phyla

**Resources:** Silverthorn for physiology (this is where Campbell's depth often falls short). Hickman's Zoology for comparative anatomy if needed.

### Month 3: Plant Biology and Ecology

**Week 9-10: Plant physiology and anatomy**
- Photosynthesis: light reactions at the PS I/II level, Calvin cycle, photorespiration, C3/C4/CAM
- Phytohormones: auxin, cytokinin, gibberellin, ethylene, abscisic acid -- mechanisms, not just names
- Water transport: water potential, cohesion-tension theory
- Root, stem, leaf cross-section anatomy; secondary growth

**Week 11-12: Ecology and evolution**
- Population dynamics: exponential vs logistic growth, carrying capacity, r/K selection
- Community ecology: competition, predation, mutualism, succession
- Population genetics: Hardy-Weinberg (quantitative problems), drift, founder effect, bottleneck
- Speciation mechanisms

**Resources:** Taiz for plant physiology depth. Campbell for ecology and evolution.

**Start past papers this month** if you haven't already. One paper every two weeks, timed. Don't worry about score -- focus on identifying which topics cost you marks.

### Month 4: Fill gaps + remaining sections

- [Ethology](/resources/9-ethology/): behavioral ecology, mating systems, foraging strategies, kin selection
- [Bioinformatics](/resources/10-bioinformatics/): BLAST, sequence alignment, phylogenetic tree reading
- Review and fill any gaps identified by past-paper analysis

This month is also when you should revisit Cell Biology and Genetics -- you studied them 3 months ago, and spaced repetition matters. Spend 2-3 hours per week reviewing these foundational topics alongside new material.

### Months 5-6: Intensive practice

**Month 5:** One past paper per week plus targeted revision on persistent weak areas. After each paper:

1. Score it
2. List every wrong answer by topic
3. For the 3 topics that cost you the most marks, re-study that section for 2-3 hours
4. Repeat with the next paper

**Month 6:** Two papers per week under full timed conditions. Between papers, review errors only -- no new content. This is about building exam fluency: speed, accuracy, and time management under pressure.

## Weekly rhythm

Ten to twelve hours a week. Suggested structure:

| Day | Activity | Duration |
|---|---|---|
| Monday | Concept study (new material or targeted review) | 2 hrs |
| Wednesday | Concept study | 2 hrs |
| Friday | Problem-solving (end-of-chapter questions, topic-specific drills) | 2 hrs |
| Saturday or Sunday | Past-paper attempt (timed) + error analysis | 3-4 hrs |

Consistency matters more than any single heroic session. Missing a Monday session and doing 4 hours on Saturday instead is worse than doing 2+2.

## What to deprioritize

Don't linger on any single subtopic. Read for understanding, take notes, do a few practice problems, move on. You can come back during months 5-6 if a topic keeps hurting your paper scores. Specifically:

- Detailed invertebrate taxonomy beyond major phyla
- Deep ethology beyond core behavioral ecology concepts
- Bioinformatics beyond BLAST basics (unless you're targeting IBO specifically)

## Resources

- **Campbell Biology** as the core throughout
- **Silverthorn** for animal physiology depth
- **Taiz** for plant physiology depth
- **Lehninger** for biochemistry calculations
- Grade S books, read selectively rather than cover-to-cover. The [Study Resources page](/resources/) has category filters to find topic-specific books quickly.
- [Past papers](/papers/) from month 3 onward
