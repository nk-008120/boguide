# USABO Preparation: The Complete Guide

The USA Biology Olympiad is the US national selection pathway for [IBO](/ibo-preparation/), run by the Center for Excellence in Education. Around 10,000 students sit the Open Exam each year; roughly 240 advance to the Semifinal, 20 reach the National Finals, and 4 represent the US at IBO. This guide focuses on preparation strategy. For current registration windows, exact dates, and school-registration procedures, always check the [official USABO site](https://www.usabo-trc.org/), because those details change every year.

## The three stages

### Open Exam

The Open Exam is the entry point. It's a 50-minute multiple-choice/true-false test taken at your school under teacher supervision. There are typically around 50 questions.

**What the Open tests:** The Open is designed to be accessible to students who have taken AP Biology, but it tests content well beyond the AP syllabus. Questions are factual and concept-based, and they cover the full breadth of the IBO syllabus -- cell biology, genetics, physiology, ecology, evolution, and systematics all appear.

**The bar:** Typically, scoring in the top ~240 students nationally (roughly the top 2-3%) earns a Semifinal invitation. The exact cutoff varies each year.

The Open rewards breadth over depth. You don't need to understand every mechanism at the molecular level -- you need to know enough about enough topics. This makes it the most "studyable" stage: read widely, cover your gaps, and the Open is reachable.

### Semifinal Exam

The Semifinal is where USABO becomes a real olympiad. It's longer (around 2 hours), and the question format shifts significantly:

| Feature | Open | Semifinal |
|---|---|---|
| Length | ~50 min | ~120 min |
| Format | Multiple-choice / true-false | Multiple-choice + free-response + lab |
| Depth | Broad survey | Deep dives on fewer topics |
| Data interpretation | Minimal | Central -- figures, tables, experimental setups |
| Free response | None | Multi-part written answers |

**What the Semifinal tests:** The Semifinal goes deeper on molecular and cellular biology, genetics, and physiology. It also introduces lab-technique questions -- not hands-on lab work, but questions about experimental design, technique selection, and data interpretation from experiments you're expected to understand conceptually.

**Free-response strategy:** The free-response section is where most Semifinalists feel underprepared. Unlike multiple-choice, free response requires you to construct explanations, draw diagrams, propose experiments, or walk through calculations. You're graded on reasoning, not just the final answer. Practice writing clear, structured biological explanations -- this is a skill distinct from knowing the biology.

**The bar:** The top ~20 Semifinal scorers are invited to National Finals.

### National Finals

National Finals is a roughly two-week residential program, typically held at a university campus. It includes both theory exams and practical lab exams. The four students who perform best overall are selected for the US IBO team.

At the Finals level, preparation is more about deepening mastery than covering new ground. If you've reached Finals, you already know the material -- the question is how precisely and quickly you can apply it under pressure. This guide focuses on Open and Semifinal preparation, which is what the vast majority of USABO students are working toward.

## What gets tested: topic breakdown

USABO draws from the same IBO syllabus used worldwide, but historically certain areas appear more heavily at specific stages.

### High-frequency across both stages

- **Cell Biology**: membrane transport, organelle function, cell signaling pathways, cell cycle and mitosis/meiosis
- **Genetics**: Mendelian genetics with extensions (epistasis, linkage, sex-linkage), molecular genetics (replication, transcription, translation), gene regulation
- **Animal Physiology**: cardiovascular, respiratory, renal, endocrine, and nervous systems; homeostatic mechanisms
- **Biochemistry**: enzyme kinetics, metabolic pathways (glycolysis, Krebs cycle, oxidative phosphorylation, photosynthesis)

### Heavier at Semifinal

- **Molecular Biology techniques**: PCR, gel electrophoresis, restriction enzymes, cloning, CRISPR -- you won't run these in the exam, but you need to know how they work and how to interpret their outputs
- **Experimental design**: identifying controls, predicting outcomes, interpreting novel data from techniques you should recognize
- **Data interpretation**: reading dose-response curves, growth curves, electrophoresis gels, phylogenetic trees, sequence alignments

### Moderate frequency

- **Plant Physiology**: photosynthesis mechanisms, hormone-mediated growth, water transport
- **Ecology**: population dynamics (logistic growth, carrying capacity), community interactions, ecosystem energetics
- **Evolution**: natural selection, population genetics (Hardy-Weinberg), speciation mechanisms

### Lower frequency

- **Systematics**: phylogenetic tree reading, classification principles
- **Ethology**: behavioral ecology basics
- **Plant and animal anatomy**: comparative morphology, tissue identification

### The AP Biology overlap -- and where USABO goes further

If you've taken AP Biology, you have roughly 60-70% of the knowledge needed for the Open. The gaps are:

| AP covers well | USABO goes deeper |
|---|---|
| Cell respiration overview | Full enzyme kinetics, Michaelis-Menten, allosteric regulation |
| Basic genetics | Linkage mapping, epistasis, epigenetics, gene regulation at promoter level |
| Organ system overview | Detailed renal physiology (nephron mechanics), cardiac electrophysiology, immune cell types and activation |
| Photosynthesis basics | C3/C4/CAM comparison, light reaction stoichiometry, photorespiration |
| Natural selection concept | Hardy-Weinberg quantitative problems, drift, bottleneck, founder effect |

Closing these gaps is the fastest path from AP Biology to USABO Open success.

## How to prepare

### For the Open Exam

**Timeline:** 3-6 months is typical. The Open is held in February, so students usually start preparing in the fall.

**Strategy:** Cover breadth first. The Open rewards knowing a little about everything over knowing a lot about a few topics.

1. **Start with Campbell Biology.** Read it cover to cover if time allows, or at least the cell biology, genetics, physiology, and ecology sections. Campbell is pitched at the right level for Open preparation -- slightly above AP Biology.

2. **Fill AP gaps.** Use the table above to identify topics your AP course skimmed over. Enzyme kinetics, renal physiology, and plant physiology are the most common blind spots.

3. **Practice with released USABO questions.** The official site periodically releases sample Open questions. Work through these under timed conditions to calibrate your pace.

4. **Cross-train on [IBO past papers](/papers/ibo/).** IBO theory questions are harder than Open questions, but the topic coverage overlaps heavily. If you can answer 40-50% of an IBO paper correctly, you're well-positioned for the Open.

5. **Use the [Study Resources page](/resources/)** to drill specific weak areas. The 15 sections correspond to the IBO syllabus sections -- work through the ones where you're least confident.

### For the Semifinal

**Timeline:** You find out you've qualified for the Semifinal roughly 4-6 weeks before the exam. This is a short window. Students who planned ahead -- hoping for qualification and studying beyond Open level -- have a significant advantage.

**Strategy:** Shift from breadth to depth. The Semifinal tests fewer topics but goes much deeper.

1. **Upgrade your references.** Campbell alone is not enough for Semifinal.

   | Area | Book | Why it matters for Semifinal |
   |---|---|---|
   | Cell Biology | Alberts, *Molecular Biology of the Cell* | Signal transduction, cell cycle regulation at the molecular level |
   | Biochemistry | Lehninger, *Principles of Biochemistry* | Enzyme kinetics calculations, metabolic pathway regulation |
   | Animal Physiology | Silverthorn, *Human Physiology* | Integrative physiology -- the kind of multi-system reasoning Semifinal tests |
   | Genetics | Griffiths, *Introduction to Genetic Analysis* | Linkage problems, gene regulation models |

   You don't need to read these cover to cover. Target the chapters that correspond to high-frequency Semifinal topics.

2. **Practice free-response writing.** This is the skill most students neglect. Take a past IBO theory question and write out your answer in full sentences, as if explaining it to someone. Then check whether your explanation is complete, accurate, and clearly structured.

3. **Learn lab techniques conceptually.** You won't pipette in the Semifinal, but you need to know:
   - How gel electrophoresis works and how to interpret gel images
   - What PCR does and what results look like
   - How restriction enzymes cut and how to predict fragment sizes
   - How CRISPR works at a mechanistic level
   - Basic chromatography and spectrophotometry

4. **Practice data interpretation daily.** Pull a figure from a research paper (even one in a field you don't know) and practice describing what the data shows, what the controls are, and what conclusions are supported. This is a transferable skill that Semifinal questions test heavily.

5. **Time yourself.** The Semifinal is longer than the Open but covers fewer questions at much greater depth. Practice budgeting time between multiple-choice (faster) and free-response (slower, and where most students run out of time).

## Registration

USABO registration goes through your school, not through individual sign-up. Here's the process:

1. **Your school's biology teacher** registers the school through the [official USABO site](https://www.usabo-trc.org/). There is typically a registration fee.
2. **Students register with their teacher**, who administers the Open Exam at the school.
3. If your school doesn't participate: talk to your biology teacher and show them the registration page. Most teachers are willing to register once they understand the process.
4. **Homeschooled students** should check the USABO site for current participation options, which vary year to year.

The registration window typically opens in the fall for a February Open Exam. Don't miss it -- late registration is usually not available.

## Common mistakes

**Studying only what AP covered.** AP Biology gives you a head start, but it's not sufficient. The topics USABO tests beyond AP -- enzyme kinetics, renal physiology, gene regulation, plant hormones -- are not "bonus" material. They're standard exam content.

**Ignoring the format shift at Semifinal.** Students who breeze through the Open with pure factual recall often struggle when the Semifinal asks them to write out reasoning, interpret unfamiliar data, or propose an experiment. Practice the Semifinal format specifically.

**Starting Semifinal prep only after qualifying.** The 4-6 week window between Open results and the Semifinal is not enough time to learn new material in depth. Students who make Finals typically start preparing at Semifinal level from the beginning, treating the Open as something they'll pass along the way.

**Neglecting plant biology.** US students typically have much stronger animal physiology backgrounds than plant backgrounds. USABO tests plant physiology, plant hormones, and photosynthesis mechanisms regularly. Don't skip it.

**Not practicing under time pressure.** Both the Open (50 min) and Semifinal (~120 min) are time-constrained. Practice with a timer from the start.

## Past papers and practice

USABO sample questions are periodically released on the [official site](https://www.usabo-trc.org/). Cross-training on [IBO past papers](/papers/ibo/) through BiOrchive is also valuable -- the IBO theory format overlaps meaningfully with USABO Semifinal, and the difficulty calibration helps.

The [Question Bank](/papers/question-bank/) lets you filter by topic if you want to drill specific weak areas without committing to a full paper.
