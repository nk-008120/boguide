Humans have about 20,000 protein-coding genes. Assume the average (mean) human protein weighs 50 kDa. The average amino acid weighs 110 Da. Use this information to estimate the total length, in base-pairs, of coding sequence in the human genome.

In chimpanzees, < 1% of the genome is protein-coding. Centromeres are about 0.2–7 Mbp, depending on chromosome. Telomeres are about 5–15 Kbp at birth.

An alien plant has 100 million base-pairs of protein-coding sequence in its genome. The alien uses 20 different amino acids (like us) but has 3 different DNA base pairs (A/T, G/C and X/Y).
