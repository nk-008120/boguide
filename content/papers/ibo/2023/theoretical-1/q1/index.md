Skeletal muscle can be active over short or long times. There are several energy sources, each affecting the strength and endurance of muscles.

![Energy contribution curves A-D over time, with E (Energy) on the y-axis and t (Time) on the x-axis. X marks Minutes, Y marks Hours. Curve A peaks sharply within the first minute then drops. Curve B peaks around 2-4 minutes then declines. Curve C rises slowly and plateaus at a high level over hours. Curve D rises after A and B subside and sustains at a moderate level.](/papers/ibo/2023/q1-figure-1.png)
*E = Energy, t = Time, X = Minutes, Y = Hours.*

Match the corresponding curves (A-D), showing the contribution of each energy source to power generation, with the corresponding energy source (1-4):

1. Aerobic oxidation: muscle glycogen, plasma glucose or liver glycogen
2. Aerobic oxidation: adipose tissue triglycerides and plasma fatty acids
3. ATP + creatine phosphate
4. Anaerobic oxidation: muscle glycogen
