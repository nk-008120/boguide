Extrafloral nectaries (EFNs) are nectar-secreting glands located outside of flowers. They attract ants and other predatory insects that defend the plant against herbivores. This is an example of biotic defense.

Plants can also invest in mechanical defense, such as tough leaves with low specific leaf area (SLA). Low SLA indicates thicker, more structurally reinforced leaves.

The figures show data comparing herbivory levels, SLA, and other traits between species with and without EFNs in a tropical community.
