The Michaelis-Menten equation describes the relationship between substrate concentration ([S]) and enzymatic reaction rate (v).

K<sub>m</sub> = the Michaelis constant, which is the substrate concentration at which v = ½V<sub>max</sub> and represents the affinity of enzymes to particular substrates.

V<sub>max</sub> = the maximum rate in a given reaction mixture.

Glucose oxidase catalyses the oxidation of glucose (MW 180 g/mol) to glucono-1,5-lactone, while O<sub>2</sub> is reduced to H<sub>2</sub>O<sub>2</sub>. For glucose oxidase K<sub>m</sub> is estimated as 25 mmol/l.

![Michaelis-Menten equation, plot, and glucose oxidase reaction scheme showing glucose meter strip operation](/papers/ibo/2023/t2-q17-figure-1.png)

A reaction catalysed by glucose oxidase is used to measure blood glucose concentration. A glucose meter is actually an electric current meter. The blood sample is applied to a strip containing immobilized glucose oxidase, and the meter records the flow of electrons accompanying the oxidation of H<sub>2</sub>O<sub>2</sub> catalysed by a non-enzymatic catalyst contained in the meter strip.
