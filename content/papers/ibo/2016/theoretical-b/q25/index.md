In a simple way, respiratory disorders can be classified as obstructive or restrictive
disorders. Obstructive disorders are characterized by a reduction in the airflow rate
in the respiratory tracts. Restrictive disorders are characterized by a reduction of
lung volume.

Figure Q.75 shows the shapes of the flow-volume loops measured during forced
inspiration and forced expiration in healthy people with normal respiratory function
and in four patients suffering from four common types of respiratory disorders
(Type 1 through Type 4).
