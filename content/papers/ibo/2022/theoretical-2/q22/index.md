Neurons interact with each other to form the complex circuits that underlie brain function. This process is conventionally divided into three steps: (1) axons find their way to appropriate target areas (axon guidance), (2) they choose appropriate synaptic partners within those areas (synaptic specificity), and (3) functional synapses are formed (synaptogenesis).

![Five-panel diagram of mechanisms of synaptic specificity. Panel 1: an axon faces a choice between several potential synaptic partners. Panel 2 (Adhesion): matching cell-recognition proteins on the axon and one dendrite bind and adhere. Panel 3 (Repulsion): a mismatched partner is actively repelled away. Panel 4 (Elimination): an initially formed but non-functional connection is retracted/eliminated. Panel 5 (Subcellular localization): the axon terminal restricts its branching to one narrow stratum within the dendritic target field rather than spreading across the whole target area.](/papers/ibo/2022/q72-figure-1.png)
