It is known that the humoral immune system responds to antigens entering an organism by producing antibodies. The diagram shows the immune response (production of antibodies) during the initial (first) and repeated (secondary) introduction of the antigen. Note that the time from infection to the presence of a significant amount of antibody in the bloodstream is called a latent period.

![Graph of relative antibody production (logarithmic scale) versus time in days. After the first injection of antigen A at day 0, a slow-rising, low-magnitude "primary response to antigen A" peaks around day 15-17 and then declines. At day 40, a second injection of antigen A together with a first injection of antigen B is given. The response to antigen A now rises rapidly to a much higher peak (a "secondary response to antigen A"), while the response to antigen B shows the same slow, low-magnitude kinetics as a first-time ("primary") response, peaking later and much lower than the secondary response to A.](/papers/ibo/2022/q79-figure-1.png)
