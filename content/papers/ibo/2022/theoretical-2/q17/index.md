Water that is absorbed by the roots of a plant, moves from the roots to the xylem. The figure shows these pathways in the root of *Ranunculus acris*.

![Diagram of a root cross-section showing soil particles feeding into rows of cells (each drawn with a cell wall, cytoplasm, and vacuole). Two routes are traced across the cells toward a xylem vessel: the F pathway (hollow arrow), which cuts through the cell interior at each cell, and the G pathway (solid arrow), which stays within the cell-wall space around the cell corners. Cell E, at the boundary nearest the xylem vessel, is marked with dark bands (a Casparian strip) where both pathways are forced to cross into the cytoplasm before reaching the xylem vessel.](/papers/ibo/2022/q67-figure-1.png)
