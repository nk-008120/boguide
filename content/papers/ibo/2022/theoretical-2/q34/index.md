Males from inbred lines of rats with spontaneous hypertension (SHR) and normal blood pressure (WKY) were separated into 5 groups and received different diets for 10 weeks. The first group of each line received standard food (SF), groups 2, 3, 4 received SF + a different fatty acid, group 5 received SF+sucrose. In groups 2–5, the added component comprised 11% of the energy value of the SF.

| Line | Diet | Change in body mass (%) | Systolic pressure, 0 week (mmHg) | Systolic pressure, 10 weeks (mmHg) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| WKY | SF+trans-fat | 14.7 ± 1.3 | 122.2 ± 0.6 | 123.1 ± 0.6 |
| WKY | SF+sucrose | 14.9 ± 2.4 | 123.2 ± 2.0 | 123.6 ± 2.2 |
| SHR | SF | 14.2 ± 2.0 | 121.3 ± 2.1 | 121.9 ± 1.6 |
| SHR | SF+butter | 18.7 ± 1.3 | 122.1 ± 0.9 | 125.6 ± 1.3 |
| SHR | SF+trans-fat | 10.1 ± 0.3 | 200.1 ± 1.8 | 199.9 ± 0.8 |
| SHR | SF+sucrose | 20.6 ± 3.3** | 189.3 ± 3.6 | 190.1 ± 3.9 |

*Table 1. Measurements of body mass and blood pressure of the caudal artery were measured at the start and end of the experiments (selected rows; some SF/palm-oil rows in the original are not fully legible in the source scan).*

WKY palm oil and trans-fat groups show a significant decrease relative to control; SHR butter and trans-fat groups show a significant increase relative to control.](/papers/ibo/2022/q84-figure-1.png)
*Figure 1. Ratio of the heart mass to total body mass in different dietary regimes. A – WKY group, B – SHR group. * p<0.05, ** p<0.01.*

Using the information and the data in the table and figure above, determine which of the statements are true or which are false.
