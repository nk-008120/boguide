There are glycoproteins on the surface of human red blood cells with antigenic properties called H-antigen. The A allele encodes an enzyme converting part of the H-antigen into A antigen (blood type A); the B allele converts it to B antigen (blood type B); the O allele encodes an inactive enzyme that doesn't modify H-antigen. In rare cases (Bombay phenotype), the initial H antigen doesn't appear on erythrocytes at all — even with A and B alleles present, no A or B antigens are produced. Synthesis of the H antigen is encoded by the H gene, whose recessive allele is h.
