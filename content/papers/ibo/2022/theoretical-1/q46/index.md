Three-toed sloths (*Bradypus* sp.), found in South and Central America, spend their entire lives in tree foliage, descending only once a week to defecate. *Cryptoses choloepi* moths live in sloth fur, defending them from insectivorous birds, and moving with sloths to lay eggs in their feces (larvae feed on the feces; adult moths climb back onto sloths). Algae of the genus *Trichophyllus* also grow in sloth fur, serving as food for the sloth and turning its fur greenish for camouflage. *Ascomycota* fungi decompose dead moth bodies in the fur, producing nutrition for the algae.
