Hypoxia is a hallmark of solid tumors and plays a critical role in different steps of tumor progression. To develop an effective treatment against malignant tumors, it is important to understand how cancer cells that develop under hypoxic conditions avoid apoptosis. The figure shows the mechanism of apoptosis.

![Pathway diagram: Hypoxia inhibits p53, p53 promotes Bax (opposed by Bcl-2), Bax triggers mitochondrial cytochrome c release, activating Apaf-1/Caspase 9, then Caspase 3/7, leading to apoptosis.](/papers/ibo/2022/q7-figure-1.png)
*Mechanism of apoptosis. p53 protein promotes the expression of Bax and its translocation from cytosol to mitochondria, which leads to mitochondrial protein cytochrome C release to the cytoplasm, activating the caspase cascade.*

Using the information and figure, determine whether the following statements are true or false:

{{< tf-quiz id="ibo2022-t1-q7" >}}
A|Hypoxia in cancer cells suppresses apoptosis.|false|The diagram shows hypoxia inhibiting p53 specifically, which would reduce apoptosis through *this one* pathway — but that's a single mechanism, not proof of the sweeping claim that hypoxia suppresses apoptosis generally. The question stem itself says cancer cells merely "avoid" apoptosis under hypoxia, implying it takes more than this one link to fully explain it — treat this as the official key's call, not a fully resolved mechanistic story.
B|Upon Bcl-2 overexpression, the integrity of the mitochondrial membranes is preserved, which in return prevents apoptosis.|true|The diagram shows Bcl-2 directly inhibiting Bax. Since Bax is what triggers mitochondrial outer membrane permeabilization, more Bcl-2 means more Bax blocked, keeping mitochondria intact and stopping cytochrome c release — directly preventing the rest of the cascade.
C|It is necessary to suppress p53 to ensure the apoptosis of cancer cells.|true|Read literally against the diagram, this is the hardest one to square: p53 is shown *driving* apoptosis (via Bax), so suppressing it looks like it should hinder, not "ensure," apoptosis through this pathway. We're reproducing the official key's answer as-is rather than a mechanistic explanation we're confident in — worth double-checking this one against the original if it matters for your prep.
D|Deficiency of Bax protein promotes apoptosis.|false|Bax is the pro-apoptotic trigger in this pathway (it's what causes mitochondrial cytochrome c release) — losing Bax would remove that trigger and *reduce* apoptosis, not promote it.
