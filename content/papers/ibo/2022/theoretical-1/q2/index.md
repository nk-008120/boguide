Solutions of a specific concentration of enzyme and substrate were mixed to record the rate of the enzymatic reaction. The time required to complete the reaction was measured at various temperatures. The enzyme is completely denatured at 50°C.

![Four candidate graphs of time-to-completion vs. temperature.](/papers/ibo/2022/q2-figure-1.png)
*Four candidate graphs of time-taken-for-completion vs. temperature.*

For each graph, determine if it represents the results of the experiment correctly (true) or not (false):

{{< tf-quiz id="ibo2022-t1-q2" >}}
A|Graph 1 (U-shaped: high time at low temperature, falling to a minimum around 25–30°C, then rising sharply toward 50°C).|true|This is the expected shape: the reaction speeds up (less time) as temperature rises toward the optimum, then slows and eventually can't complete at all as the enzyme denatures approaching 50°C.
B|Graph 2 (bell-shaped: low time at the extremes, peaking around 30–40°C).|false|The y-axis is time, so a peak at the "optimal" temperature would mean the reaction is slowest there — backwards from what actually happens at an enzyme's optimum.
C|Graph 3 (a straight line, time increasing steadily across the whole range).|false|This implies the reaction gets slower and slower from the very lowest temperature onward, with no speed-up phase at all on the way to the optimum — contradicts typical enzyme behaviour below the optimum.
D|Graph 4 (a straight line, time decreasing steadily all the way to 50°C).|false|This implies the reaction keeps getting faster right up to 50°C. But the enzyme is fully denatured at exactly 50°C, so the reaction shouldn't be able to complete at all there — time should shoot up, not reach its lowest value.
{{< /tf-quiz >}}
