Oxytocin is a neuropeptide that plays a crucial role in shaping social relations and interactions, thought to be produced in response to tactile contact. Scientists studied oxytocin release in girls aged 7–12 after a public-speaking contest (stressor). The control group immediately watched a neutral movie; the tactile group had 15 minutes of direct contact with their mothers first; the vocal group instead spoke with their mothers by phone for 15 minutes. Cortisol and oxytocin levels were measured throughout.

![Figure 1. Changes in salivary cortisol levels in girls during observations, for control, vocal, and tactile groups.](/papers/ibo/2022/q25-figure-1.png)
*Figure 1. Changes in cortisol levels during observations. Green arrow: start of public speaking. Pink arrow: end of public speaking. Black — control, Blue — vocal, Red — tactile. Cortisol rose from baseline to peak in all groups; the no-contact (control) group had higher cortisol even an hour after the stressor than the other two groups.*

![Figure 2. Urinary oxytocin content relative to creatinine, for control, vocal, and tactile groups.](/papers/ibo/2022/q25-figure-2.png)
*Figure 2. Oxytocin content in urine, relative to creatinine. Black — control, Blue — vocal, Red — tactile. Both physical and speech-only contact raised oxytocin within 15 minutes post-stressor, an effect maintained up to 1 hour post-stressor.*
