<h3>4 things the official result does not say out loud</h3>

<div class="curiosity">
  <div class="curiosity-num">01</div>
  <div class="curiosity-title">r = 0.94</div>
  <p>Theory total correlated at 0.94 with final score. No single practical station came close (the highest was Molecular Biology at 0.71). If you had to predict someone's final rank from one number, their combined theory score would be right 94% of the way there.</p>
</div>

<div class="curiosity">
  <div class="curiosity-num">02</div>
  <div class="curiosity-title">0.53 points</div>
  <p>The gap between the last Gold and the first Silver was 0.53 points. In a 165-point competition, that is 0.3% of the winner's score. A single extra True/False statement on either theory paper would have flipped the result.</p>
</div>

<div class="curiosity">
  <div class="curiosity-num">03</div>
  <div class="curiosity-title">Biochemistry was the "easy" lab</div>
  <p>With 67.7% average attainment, Biochemistry was the most accessible practical station by a wide margin. Bioinformatics (44.5%) was the hardest. The 23-percentage-point gap between the easiest and hardest station is one of the largest in recent IBO history.</p>
</div>

<div class="curiosity">
  <div class="curiosity-num">04</div>
  <div class="curiosity-title">Pengbo Li: +2.10 theory-practical gap</div>
  <p>China's #2 finisher (overall rank 2, final score 160.11) had the single largest theory-over-practical gap of any competitor. His theory T-score was 2.10 standard deviations above his own practical average, yet he still finished second overall. Pure theory dominance at the very top of the field.</p>
</div>
