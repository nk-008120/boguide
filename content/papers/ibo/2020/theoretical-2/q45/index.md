Primary succession can begin in a virtually lifeless area, characterized by early-seral plant species followed by
replacement with late-seral plant species, as seen in Alaska where glaciers have retreated during the Holocene.
Nitrogen enters soil through biological nitrogen fixation (conversion of N2 into usable forms). Phosphorus is
added to soil through weathering of rocks. After plant death, elements can reenter soil through microbial
decomposition and mineralization, but some nutrients are lost through leaching. The climax stage is boreal
forest.
