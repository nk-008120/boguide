In some plants, fruit valves bend and/or coil explosively to disperse seeds. While dehydration typically
triggers this in many species, fruit valves of Cardamine hirsuta instead bend and coil explosively upon
mechanical stimulation when the fruits are fresh and turgid. Researchers tested bending of living intact fruit
valves (all layers combined) in air, pure water, and 4 M NaCl solution; bending of freeze-thaw-killed valves in
water; and bending of living valves separated into outer-layer-only or inner-layer-only in water. Results
(Table 1): all layers combined, living, in air: no bending. All layers combined, living, in water: strong
bending. All layers combined, living, in 4 M NaCl: no bending. All layers combined, killed, in water: no
bending. Outer layer only, living, in water: bending, and it shrank longitudinally. Inner layer only, living, in
water: little bending, and its length was unchanged.
