CDK is a family of cyclin dependent kinases which drive the cell cycle. A portion of the liver was removed in WT mice, or in livers which had a particular *CDK* gene knocked-out. Mice were allowed to regenerate, then their regenerated livers were weighed, measured and stained. Individual liver cells were removed and stained for total DNA content, or for newly synthesised DNA (Figure 1).

![Five-panel figure: (top) photos of a regenerated wild-type liver vs. a CDK-/- liver, similar size; (scatter plot) liver mass at 4 and 21 days, WT and CDK-/- overlapping; (histology) H&E-stained hepatocyte nuclei, CDK-/- nuclei visibly larger; (DNA-content histogram) WT shows one main peak, CDK-/- shows several higher-ploidy peaks; (flow cytometry) newly-synthesised DNA vs. total DNA content, WT and CDK-/- patterns similar.](/papers/ibo/2019/q4-figure-1.png)
*Fig.1. Axis 1 = Liver mass. Axis 2 = DNA content. Axis 3 = # of cells. Axis 4 = DNA content. Axis 5 = Newly synthesised DNA. Legend 1 = 4 days. Legend 2 = 21 days (left side = wild-type, right side = CDK-/-).*

Indicate with an **X** whether you would expect the number or amount of each of the following structures to increase (A), not change (B), or decrease (C) in *CDK* knock-out livers versus wild-type ones.
