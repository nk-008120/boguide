Ec+ is an X-linked gene found in a newly discovered mammalian species. Females homozygous for the mutant allele (Ec) have green eyes, while a single copy of Ec+ is sufficient to result in the wild-type brown eyes.

Ec codes for an enzyme which catalyses the chemical reaction converting an inert substrate, commonly found throughout the body, into molecule X, which diffuses freely in the animal and can be detected in bodily secretions. Molecule X is a very potent epigenetic regulator, such that a few particles are sufficient to induce life-long effects on the eye phenotype.

Consider the following crossing experiments. What are the genotypes and eye colours of the progeny of animals descending from the following crosses, and what are the proportions of the various descendants?

1. Ec/Ec females × Ec+/Y males
2. Ec+/Ec females × Ec/Y males
3. Ec+/Ec females × Ec+/Y males

Use one letter from each group — (A-C) proportion, (D-H) genotype, (K-L) phenotype — in each box you fill in (three letters per box). Note that not all boxes need to be filled; if a box should remain empty, indicate that with an "X".

- A. 25%
- B. 50%
- C. 100%
- D. Ec+/Ec+ female
- E. Ec+/Ec female
- F. Ec/Ec female
- G. Ec+/Y male
- H. Ec/Y male
- K. brown eyed
- L. green eyed
