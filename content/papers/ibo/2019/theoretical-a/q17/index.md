The impact of feeding on the central circadian clock of the brain, and the peripheral clocks of other cells, was assessed. Mice were either fed only from 6.00-18.00 (daytime feeding) or only from 18.00-6.00 (nighttime feeding). The amount of Per1 and Per2 protein in liver is shown. The central brain clock was stained with labelled DNA complementary to the *per1* or *per2* sequences.

![Top: two blot panels (Nighttime feeding, Daytime feeding), each showing Per1 and Per2 protein band intensity across timepoints 6, 10, 14, 18, 22, 2 (hours). Nighttime feeding: Per1 darkest at 18, faint elsewhere; Per2 darkest at 22. Daytime feeding: Per1 darkest at 6; Per2 darkest at 10. Bottom: brain coronal section (small, greyscale) with a boxed region highlighted, plus a grid of small in-situ hybridization images for Per1 and Per2 across the same six timepoints for both feeding conditions, showing a small dark-staining structure of varying intensity per timepoint.](/papers/ibo/2019/q17-figure-1.png)
