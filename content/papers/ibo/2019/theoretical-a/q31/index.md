**Ecology**

The smallmouth bass (*Micropterus dolomieu*) is an introduced species in the area examined in the following experiment. To protect native fish species, a smallmouth bass removal program was initiated in Little Moose Lake in 2000 (indicated by the vertical dashed line). It continued until 2007. A research group examined the effect of the removal on the smallmouth bass population. They found that while the total biomass of the population decreased during the program, the population size increased. To determine the cause, they subdivided the population into three groups based on individual size: yearlings (<100mm, panel a), juveniles (100-200mm, panel b) and adults (>200mm, panel c). They collected two samples each year (spring and fall).

![Three stacked line-graph panels (a: yearlings, b: juveniles, c: adults) sharing the x-axis (year, 1998-2007) with a vertical dashed line at 2000 marking the start of the removal program. Solid lines = spring samplings, dashed lines = fall samplings, y-axis = catch per unit effort (CPUE). Panel (a) yearlings: relatively low and flat before 2000, becomes markedly higher and more variable (oscillating) after 2000, generally increasing over the program. Panel (b) juveniles: modest, roughly flat/slightly increasing throughout, with fall values pulling higher than spring toward the end. Panel (c) adults: starts moderate, peaks around 1999, then declines sharply and steadily throughout the removal program to a low, flat level by 2007.](/papers/ibo/2019/q31-figure-1.png)
*Fig.1. The change in abundance of the subpopulations (y-axis) over time (x-axis). Solid lines = spring samplings, dashed lines = fall samplings. Axis 1 = Year. Axis 2 = Catch per unit effort (CPUE).*

Which of the following models can possibly describe how the removal of individuals affected the population? Indicate with an X if each of the following models can (T) or cannot (F) describe the changes.
