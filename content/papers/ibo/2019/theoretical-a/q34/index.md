**Biosystematics**

In this task you get a few parts of a phylogenetic tree of birds, and you should assemble the full tree based on them.

![Four small cladogram fragments, each showing 5 of the 10 named bird species clustered in a different partial arrangement, illustrating overlapping subsets of the true relationships: (top-left) A. crecca separate, then P. cristatus+S. borin paired, joined with T. totanus+P. carbo paired; (top-right) A. cinerea separate, then C. monedula joined with a T. pilaris+(P. cristatus+S. borin) group; (bottom-left) C. monedula separate, then A. cinerea+P. carbo paired, joined with C. crex+L. argentatus paired; (bottom-right) T. pilaris+S. borin paired, joined with C. crex+(T. totanus+L. argentatus).](/papers/ibo/2019/q34-figure-1.png)
*Fig.1.*

The species to place in the full phylogenetic tree, indicated by letters:

- A – *A. cinerea*
- B – *A. crecca*
- C – *C. crex*
- D – *C. monedula*
- E – *L. argentatus*
- F – *P. carbo*
- G – *P. cristatus*
- H – *S. borin*
- I – *T. pilaris*
- J – *T. totanus*

You should find the place of each species in the tree below. Match the letter of the species (A-J) with the Roman numerals (I.-VII.) of the positions in the tree. One numeral can belong to more than one letter.

![A skeleton phylogenetic tree with seven labeled terminal/grouped positions (I. through VII.) arranged hierarchically: a shallow pair of "I." tips, joined with "II.", joined with a pair of "III." tips (deeper split), forming the upper major clade; separately, "IV." joins with "V.", which joins with a pair of "VI." tips, forming the lower major clade; "VII." branches off earliest, separate from both major clades.](/papers/ibo/2019/q34-figure-2.png)
*Fig.2. Target skeleton tree with positions I–VII to be filled in with species letters A–J.*

Indicate your answer by putting an X in the appropriate box on the answer sheet.
