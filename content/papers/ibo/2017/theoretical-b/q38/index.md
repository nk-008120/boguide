Peter Mitchell (1920-1992) discovered how mitochondria produce ATP. Electrons are harvested from succinate, malate and ascorbate (vitamin C), and drawn onto oxygen. Complexes I-IV sequentially harness their energy to pump protons across mitochondrial inner membranes (1). The oxygen saturation of a suspension of mitochondria, treated with substrates and the poisons potassium cyanide (KCN), rotenone or antimycin A (AA) at the indicated points, was measured over time (2).
