Cork oak (Quercus suber) produces suberin, a highly resistant material, from fatty acids, and uses it to reinforce the cell walls of cork. The expression of genes specific to cork tissue was measured across the year, and the genes were grouped into biosynthetic enzymes, or chaperone proteins. Sir Hugh Pellham (1954-present) discovered chaperones, which assist other proteins to fold properly.
