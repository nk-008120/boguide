In fruit flies, dietary content affects arousal to mechanical vibrations during sleep, a process mediated by enteroendocrine cells in the gut (Figure 1).

![Five-panel figure: panel A is a schematic of enteroendocrine (EE) cells secreting CCHa1 into circulation, which binds a CCHa1 receptor on a DA neuron that synapses onto an MBON neuron via dopamine; panel B is a bar chart of CCHa1 mRNA level under four diets (regular food, +sugar, +fat, +protein); panel C is a bar chart of percent flies awoken by stimulation, comparing control to CCHa1-depleted flies; panel D is a bar chart comparing arousal in control versus shi-ts mutant flies; panel E is a bar chart comparing arousal in control versus flies overexpressing TrpA1 in MBON neurons.](/papers/ibo/2024/theoretical-b/q49-figure-1.png)
*Figure 1. A The interaction scheme between enteroendocrine (EE) cells (secreting CCHa1) and the central nervous system. B The effect of dietary content on CCHa1 expression (Y-1: CCHa1 mRNA level in the gut; A: regular food, B: +sugar [14 kcal], C: +fat [80 kcal], D: +protein [40 kcal]). C The effect of CCHa1 depletion on arousal during sleep (Y-2: % flies awoken by stimulation). D The impact of the shi-ts mutation (defective endocytosis/exocytosis in the DA neuron) on arousal during sleep. E The impact of overexpressing TrpA1 (a cation channel transporting Na⁺ and Ca²⁺) in MBON neurons on arousal during sleep; Na⁺ and Ca²⁺ concentrations are lower inside the neuron than outside.*
