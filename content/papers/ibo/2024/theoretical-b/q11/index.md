Proteasomes, cytoplasmic multiprotein complexes, and lysosomes are major proteolysis (protein degradation) sites in eukaryotic cells.

In Figure 1-a, researchers studied the abundance of proteins A, B, and C over time in cultured human cells using cycloheximide, an inhibitor of translation elongation in eukaryotic 80S ribosomes. Note that cycloheximide does not affect the abundance of protein A, whose half-life is shorter than that of protein B.

In Figure 1-b, they observed how MG132, a proteasome inhibitor, and chloroquine, a lysosome inhibitor, affect the abundance of proteins B and C over time under cycloheximide treatment.
