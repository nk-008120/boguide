Researchers studied the effect of photoperiod and CONSTANS (CO) protein, a critical mediator of photoperiodic cues, on seed mass and size.

![Three-panel figure: panel A shows bar charts comparing days-to-flowering (DGF) and seed mass (SM) under 16-hour-day/8-hour-night versus 12-hour-day/12-hour-night conditions for Lotus japonicus and Vigna umbellata; panel B shows a bar chart of seed size at 2, 4, 6, 8, and 10 days after pollination, comparing control and Dex-treated CO-transgenic seeds, with significance brackets; panel C shows a bar chart comparing mature ovule size between wild-type and CO loss-of-function plants.](/papers/ibo/2024/theoretical-b/q36-figure-1.png)
*Figure 1. A The flowering time and seed mass of two plant species grown under 16-hour-day/8-hour-night (16-8) and 12-hour-day/12-hour-night (12-12) conditions, representing long-day and short-day photoperiods (DGF: days between germination and flowering; SM: seed mass). B How CO in seeds affects seed size (mm²) in wild-type (WT) and CO-transgenic (CO-trans) seeds, where CO is only functional upon incubating seeds with the compound Dex (DAP: days after pollination; "s"/"ns" denote significant/non-significant differences). C Mature ovule size (mm²) of wild-type (WT) and CO loss-of-function (CO-LoF) mutants.*
