Dichotomous keys are essential tools in taxonomy. They consist of a series of statements, each highlighting a distinct morphological characteristic that may be true or false depending on the organism under observation. As the organism is compared with these statements, the key systematically directs the user through a decision tree, ultimately leading to a specific taxonomic classification.

![A three-level dichotomous decision tree of circles connected by branches labeled T (true) and F (false), starting from one root circle and ending in four leaf circles at the bottom.](/papers/ibo/2024/theoretical-b/q1-figure-1.png)
*Figure 1. Dichotomous tree model with three levels; T - true, F - false.*

To determine the species of an unknown organism, referred to as X, you utilize a hypothetical dichotomous key with 10 levels, structurally similar to the one depicted in Figure 1. The final level of this tree provides a conclusive taxonomic identification, with no further branching points. The tree has a complete set of nodes without terminating branches before the final level.

On your answer sheet, indicate "T" for true statements and "F" for false ones.
