Proteins A and B form a heterodimer and only then do they induce stem cell differentiation. Protein A is regulated at the transcriptional level by transcription factor X, which is in turn regulated by the ligand L. On the other hand, protein B is regulated post-translationally through phosphorylation and dephosphorylation at serine-121 residue.

![Panel A: two Western blots of unlabeled fractions I and II, each with/without ligand L, probed for transcription factor X, Actin, and Histone H3. Panel B: a Western blot of a differentiation marker and Actin across control, kinase-deficient, and phosphatase-deficient conditions. Panel C: the chemical structures of phosphorylated serine, aspartate, and leucine.](/papers/ibo/2024/theoretical-b/q10-figure-1.png)
*Figure 1. A Researchers isolated DNA-bound and unbound proteins (fractions) and analyzed the abundance of transcription factor X by Western Blot analysis but forgot to label the fractions. B Researchers studied how the deficiency of kinase and phosphatase affects the differentiation of cells by performing a Western Blot analysis. The higher the differentiation marker the higher the chance of differentiation. C The structures of phosphorylated serine and two other amino acids. Note that one amino acid can mimic phosphorylated serine.*

On your answer sheet, indicate "T" for true statements and "F" for false ones.
