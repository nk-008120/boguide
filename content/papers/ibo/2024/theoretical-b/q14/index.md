The Aral Sea, located in the southwest of Kazakhstan, was one of the largest salt lakes in the world before drying out. Today, the Aral Sea has two basins: Northern and Southern. Freshwater rivers: Syr Darya flows into the Northern basin and Amu Darya flows into the Southern basin. The water from Amu Darya is overused in agriculture, thus only a small part flows into the sea.

One way of restoring the biota of the Aral Sea is by introducing a food chain of new organisms into the sea. Note that species W and Z are native to the Aral Sea ecosystem, and also inhabit freshwater rivers flowing into the Aral Sea.

Right: a graph of salinity and the population quantity of species W and Z over time from before 1970 to 2000s, with an arrow marking an artificial species introduction.](/papers/ibo/2024/theoretical-b/q14-figure-1.png)
