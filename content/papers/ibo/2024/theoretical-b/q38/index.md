Kazakhstan is one of the significant contributors to global wheat production and export. Researchers at Kazakh National University studied how chemical A affects wheat (*T. aestivum*) development.

![Two-panel figure: panel A shows photographs comparing seed and isolated-embryo germination with and without chemical A (Ch-A) treatment; panel B shows a zymogram gel with bands for control, Phyt-X, and Phyt-X-with-Ch-A treatments, where dark bands indicate starch digestion by α-amylase.](/papers/ibo/2024/theoretical-b/q38-figure-1.png)
*Figure 1. A The impact of chemical A (Ch-A) on the germination of seeds and isolated embryos (C: control). B The effect of chemical A on α-amylase activity in seeds (C: control). Briefly: wheat embryos were treated with chemical A and/or phytohormone X (Phyt-X), then total proteins were extracted and separated by polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (PAGE). The gel was immersed in starch solution, then iodine solution was added — iodine reacts with starch to produce a dark black color and prevents a bright white color from appearing (as in panel B); glucose does not react with iodine.*
