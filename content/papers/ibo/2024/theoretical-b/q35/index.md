Phytochrome B (phyB) is a plant photoreceptor responsible for numerous physiological aspects of plant growth. PhyB exists in two states, Pr and Pfr, and the interconversion between them is regulated by red and far-red light (Figure 1A). Interestingly, temperature can also affect the interconversion between Pr and Pfr. At the cellular level, PhyB forms structures called nuclear bodies (NB, Figure 1E).

![Five-panel figure: panel A is a schematic showing red light (R) converting Pr to Pfr and far-red light (FR) converting Pfr back to Pr; panel B plots PhyB absorbance at 725 nm over time at four temperatures (10, 20, 25, 30°C); panel C plots hypocotyl growth for wild-type versus phyB null mutant seedlings; panel D plots log nuclear-body size against log red-to-far-red light ratio; panel E shows two fluorescence micrographs of nuclear bodies (NB) in plants K and H.](/papers/ibo/2024/theoretical-b/q35-figure-1.png)
*Figure 1. A The control of PhyB states by red (R) and far-red (FR) light. B The effect of temperature on the absorbance of PhyB at 725 nm light (far-red); an equal amount of PhyB was used in each condition. C Hypocotyl growth in wild-type (WT) and phyB null mutant (mut) seedlings. D The impact of the red-to-far-red light ratio on nuclear body (NB) size in temperature-irresponsive PhyB mutants (only light controls Pr/Pfr interconversion in these mutants). E The NBs from two different wild-type plants (K and H) grown under different conditions.*
