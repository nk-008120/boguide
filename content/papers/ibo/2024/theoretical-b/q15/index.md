The Lotka-Volterra model is used to predict the dynamics of competing populations. It is based on the following equations and is analyzed by constructing specific lines:

$$\frac{dN_1}{dt} = r_1 N_1 \left(\frac{K_1 - N_1 - \alpha N_2}{K_1}\right), \qquad \frac{dN_2}{dt} = r_2 N_2 \left(\frac{K_2 - N_2 - \beta N_1}{K_2}\right)$$

where K - carrying capacity, N - number of individuals, r - intrinsic rate of increase, α - competition coefficient of species 2 on species 1, β - competition coefficient of species 1 on species 2, and dN/dt = population growth rate.

The Lotka-Volterra line represents the number of individuals in a population at equilibrium (dN/dt = 0). For two species in competition, there are only 4 combinations of the location of these lines. Depending on the initial values of populations, the future changes in populations' dynamics can be predicted.
