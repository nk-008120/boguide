Intratumor heterogeneity refers to distinct tumor cell populations within the same patient's tumor. During tumor evolution, random mutations in genes (A-J) of different tumor cells result in distinct populations of cells (R1-R4) with different genetic and phenotypic profiles.

![Panel A: a hypothetical tumor sample divided into four colored regions R1-R4. Panel B: an evolutionary tree showing mutations A-J branching into the four regions, with A on the trunk. Panel C: a copy-number plot along a chromosome showing a dip at gene E and a spike at gene F.](/papers/ibo/2024/theoretical-b/q26-figure-1.png)
*Figure 1. A. A hypothetical tumor sample. B. The evolutionary relationship between the tumor regions of the sample, shown in A. Labels A-J refer to random mutations in genes of different tumor cells. C. The copy number alterations of genes E and F (the number of times a gene is present in the genome) along the chromosome (X: position along the chromosome, Y: copy number) from the tumor sample, shown in A. Note that the genetic defects related to genes E and F are known to be due to copy number alterations.*

On your answer sheet, indicate "T" for true statements and "F" for false ones.
