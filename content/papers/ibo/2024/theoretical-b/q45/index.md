Lung volumes can change in Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (COPD). Figure 1 summarizes normal lung volumes and the changes that occur at different stages of COPD. Figure 1C shows the pressure-volume curve with end-expiratory lung volume (EELV) values for two individuals (1 and 2).

Lung volumes of mild, moderate, and severe COPD patients were studied at rest, at 1/3 maximal power output (Wmax), and at 2/3 Wmax (Figure 2). Graphs T, E, Z do not necessarily correspond to mild, moderate, and severe states in this order.

![Three-panel figure: panel A is a time-course trace of lung volume with EELV and TLC marked, alongside a stacked bar of lung volume subdivisions (ERV, FRC, VT, IC, IRV, VC, RV, TLC); panel B compares stacked lung-volume bars for mild (L), moderate (M), and severe (S) COPD; panel C is a pressure-volume (sigmoid) curve plotting volume % of vital capacity against pressure, with two tidal-volume loops marked 1 and 2 at different points on the curve.](/papers/ibo/2024/theoretical-b/q45-figure-1.png)
*Figure 1. A ERV — expiratory reserve volume; FRC — functional residual capacity; IC — inspiratory capacity; IRV — inspiratory reserve volume; RV — residual volume; TLC — total lung capacity; VC — vital capacity; VT — tidal volume. B Lung volume changes in mild (L), moderate (M), and severe (S) COPD patients. C Pressure-volume curve with end-expiratory lung volume (EELV) for two individuals (1 and 2).*

![Three-panel figure showing end-inspiratory and end-expiratory lung volume as a percentage of TLC across rest, 1/3 Wmax, and 2/3 Wmax exercise intensities, for three unlabeled COPD severity groups named T, E, and Z.](/papers/ibo/2024/theoretical-b/q45-figure-2.png)
*Figure 2. End-expiratory and end-inspiratory volumes at rest (R), 1/3 Wmax, and 2/3 Wmax. Y — volume % of TLC. Panels T, E, and Z represent three COPD severity groups, not necessarily in mild/moderate/severe order.*
