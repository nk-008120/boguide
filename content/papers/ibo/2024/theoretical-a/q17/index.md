Water is a common renewable resource on Earth; however, after its use, water loses some of its properties, such as purity, heat content, and potential energy. While they eventually will be restored, this happens on a geological timescale. Less than 1% of total water is available for human use - renewable freshwater resources (RFWR) - and even from this small fraction of water, only 10% is enough to fulfill the needs of humanity. While theoretically, there is more than enough water, many regions still experience a *water crisis* - a lack of accessible RFWR or an inability to utilize available RFWR.

![World map colored by water scarcity index (Rws) value from 0.00 (white) to 1.00 (dark red), with the highest values concentrated in the western USA, the Middle East, South/Central Asia, and eastern China.](/papers/ibo/2024/q17-figure-1.png)
*Figure 1. The world map with Rws values.*

$$R_{ws} = \frac{W - S}{Q} \quad (1)$$

$$A_w = \frac{Q}{C} \quad (2)$$

Equations: R<sub>ws</sub> - water scarcity index, A<sub>w</sub> - water crowding indicator, W - total annual water withdrawal by population, S - total water use from desalinated water, Q - RFWR, C - population size.
