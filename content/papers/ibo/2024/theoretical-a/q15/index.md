Walker circulation is the model that describes how temperature differences across the Pacific Ocean can explain local pressure and precipitation: warm, moist air rises and dries out over the Western Pacific, flows east, becomes cold and dry and sinks over the Eastern Pacific, then returns west warming and moistening again.

![Diagram of the Walker Circulation loop across the Pacific Ocean: warm moist air rising and drying over the Western Pacific, flowing east aloft, sinking as cold dry air over the Eastern Pacific, then returning west along the surface, warming and moistening again.](/papers/ibo/2024/q15-figure-1.png)
*Figure 1. Walker Circulation.*

Alterations in Walker Circulation are called El Niño Southern Oscillation (ENSO). This oscillation has 3 phases: Neutral (normal Walker Circulation), El Niño (weak or reversed Walker Circulation), and La Niña (strong Walker Circulation).
