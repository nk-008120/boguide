The Pentose Phosphate Pathway (PPP) is a metabolic pathway occurring in the cytoplasm that mainly generates NADPH and ribose 5-phosphate. NADPH is used for the reduction of reactive oxygen species (ROS) such as hydrogen peroxide. PPP has oxidative and non-oxidative phases, which are irreversible and reversible, respectively.

![Reaction diagram. Part A (oxidative, irreversible): G6P is converted via G6PDH to C6P then C5P, releasing two NADPH and one CO2. Part B (non-oxidative, reversible): C5P interconverts with R5P and, via two transketolase (TKT) reactions, with C7P, GA3P, C4P, and F6P, ultimately yielding F6P and GA3P.](/papers/ibo/2024/q7-figure-1.png)
*Figure 1. A Oxidative. B Non-oxidative. G6P – glucose 6-phosphate, GA3P – glyceraldehyde 3-phosphate, F6P – fructose 6-phosphate, R5P – ribose 5-phosphate, G6PDH – glucose 6-phosphate dehydrogenase, TKT – transketolase, N - nucleotides. CXP - other intermediates where X is the position of phosphorylated carbon. Note that all intermediates of PPP are phosphorylated on the last carbon.*
