During pulmonary function tests, tidal volume, inspiratory reserve volume, and expiratory reserve volume can be measured directly, but not residual volume (RV). The helium dilution technique estimates RV (Figure 1), by recording Vsf (final spirometer volume) and C2 (final helium concentration). The initial spirometer volume is 5 L with 10% helium. Helium is not exchanged in the lungs, i.e. not absorbed into the blood.

![Diagram of the helium dilution test: a patient attached to a spirometer after regular expiration, then performing a full forced expiration, alongside a table of Vsf and final helium concentration for a healthy control and two patients.](/papers/ibo/2024/q48-figure-1.png)
*Figure 1. A In the first stage, the patient is attached to the spirometer after regular expiration and then breathes several times to establish helium equilibrium. Vs - initial spirometer volume. B The patient performs a full forced expiration, and the spirometer is detached. Table 1. Results of the helium dilution test for two male patients and one male control: Vsf (L) / C2 (%) — Healthy control 6.1 / 6.8, Patient 1: 5.6 / 6.5, Patient 2: 5.6 / 8.*
