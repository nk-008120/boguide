Mass extinctions occur when environmental changes outpace rates of species adaptation. One way to quantify this change is by measuring the addition of inorganic carbon to oceans over time. Significant changes in the flux between inorganic and organic carbon reservoirs are termed carbon cycle perturbation events. Mass change measures the magnitude of change in inorganic carbon mass in oceans. Another quantifying metric of a carbon cycle, isotopic shift measures the change in carbon isotopic composition during the event.

In the last 220 million years, the biological pump, which transports organic carbon and calcium carbonate from the ocean's surface to the deep sea, has significantly strengthened.
