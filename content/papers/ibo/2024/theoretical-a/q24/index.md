While central dogma claims that synonymous substitutions (which do not change the primary sequence of the polypeptide) do not have any effect on protein sequence and its cellular function, codons are not used to the same extent in the vast majority of sequenced genomes. This is known as the codon usage bias. For example, among four codons that encode glycine in *Escherichia coli*, GGU and GGC have higher abundance than GGA and GGG codons. The primary driver for such unequal codon usage is to match the available pool of tRNA molecules.
