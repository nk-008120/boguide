Kazakhstan boasts a rich ecosystem harboring numerous species of tulips, some of which are direct ancestors to many modern cultivated varieties. These tulips exhibit a diverse range of morphological traits, although not all of these traits are reliable indicators of species identification.

In Table 1 below, we outline five distinct types of tulips and their variations across five characteristics (A-E), typically utilized in the classification of tulip types.

![Table showing photos of five tulip species - Tulipa lemmersii, T. salsola, T. altacia, T. bifloroformis, T. borszczowii - with a grid of five character states (A: stamen hairs, B: flower number, C: bulb hairs, D: leaf number, E: filament color) below each photo.](/papers/ibo/2024/q1-figure-1.png)
*Table 1. Morphological comparisons; A - presence/absence of hairs on the stamen, B - number of flowers (2(+) = 2 or more), C - presence/absence of a dense layer of hairs on the bulbs, D - number of leaves, E - color of the stamen filament, Y - yellow, K - black.*

It's worth noting that *T. borszczowii* has the same features as the common ancestor among these tulip species.

On your answer sheet, indicate "T" for true statements and "F" for false ones.
