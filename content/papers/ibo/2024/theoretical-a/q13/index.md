Legumes can fix nitrogen from the air. Anecdotal evidence showed industrial pollution with nitrogen and other macronutrients making legumes less fit for nitrogen-poor steppes and other grasslands, which make up half of Kazakhstan.

![Six bar-chart panels (2 rows x 3 columns) showing normalized change (NC) in legume cover (A), species richness (B), and biomass (C) after 3 years (top row) and 6 years (bottom row) of soil treatment with combinations of nitrogen (N), phosphorus (P), and potassium (K+), relative to control soil (CTL). Black dots mark bars that are statistically significant versus control; nitrogen-containing treatments are consistently negative.](/papers/ibo/2024/q13-figure-1.png)
*Figure 1. The graph shows the effect of increased nutrients on the legumes in steppe field plot study. They had control soil (CTL), as well as soil enriched with N (nitrogen), P (phosphorus), K⁺ (potassium), and combinations of these elements. The top row is the normalized change (NC) after 3 years of treatment, and the bottom row is the NC after 6 years of treatment. Letters represent NC in A) legume cover B) species richness C) biomass of legumes. Black dots represent a statistically significant difference from control. Whiskers stand for the Standard Error of Mean (SEM).*

On your answer sheet, indicate "T" for true statements and "F" for false ones.
