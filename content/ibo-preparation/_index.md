# IBO Preparation: The Complete Guide

The International Biology Olympiad is the global final stage: national teams of four secondary-school students compete on both theory and practical biology. This guide covers preparation strategy. For eligibility, current dates, and official rules, always check your national body's site and the [IBO official site](https://www.ibo-info.org/), because administrative details change every year.

## Format

IBO runs across roughly a week, with two full days of theory papers and separate days of practical work. Theory papers are typically multiple-select or true/false format at high density. Practicals cover a rotating mix of subjects across four labs, often including plant anatomy, animal anatomy, bioinformatics, and one lab that changes year to year.

## What gets tested

IBO expects genuine breadth across the whole syllabus. The [15-section learning path](/resources/#browse-all-subjects) reflects the topics you should be comfortable with. Beyond that, IBO leans heavily on:

- **Data interpretation**: reading experimental data, spotting flaws, drawing defensible conclusions.
- **Comparative biology**: contrasting plant and animal systems, comparative anatomy and physiology across taxa.
- **Cross-topic synthesis**: questions that combine, for example, biochemistry with plant physiology, or genetics with evolution.

The [topic depth map](/biology-olympiad-preparation/#how-deep-do-you-need-to-go) in the main preparation guide shows which subtopics IBO expects you to know deeply versus superficially.

## How to prepare

Route depends on how much time you have:

- **Two years or more**: follow the [2-year study plan](/plans/2-years/). Full syllabus mastery is achievable.
- **One year**: the [1-year study plan](/plans/1-year/) is the default for serious IBO prep.
- **Six months, foundations already in place**: the [6-month plan](/plans/6-months/) covers the syllabus at moderate depth.
- **Three months**: the [3-month plan](/plans/3-months/) prioritizes high-frequency topics honestly. You will lose some marks on rarer subtopics.

For any timeframe, Grade S books on the [Study Resources page](/resources/) are the standard reference set. Alberts for cell biology, Lehninger for biochemistry, Campbell for general coverage, Taiz for plant physiology.

## Practical preparation

The practical section is where students without lab access struggle most. If your school doesn't have a lab:

- **Bioinformatics practicals**: work through the [Bioinformatics section](/resources/10-bioinformatics/) and try the [IBO 2022 bioinformatics simulator](/ibo2022-sim.html).
- **Plant computational biology**: try the [Plant Computational Biology section](/resources/14-practical-4/) with its interactive simulators.
- **Wet-lab practicals**: BiOBytes' [lab recommendations article](/biobytes/articles/lab-recommendations/) covers what you can practice at home versus what genuinely needs lab access.

## Past papers

[BiOrchive](/papers/) hosts IBO past papers with an interactive attempt mode. Start attempting papers under timed conditions once you've covered roughly two-thirds of the syllabus. Every mistake becomes a targeted mini-revision.

## National selection

IBO teams are selected through national olympiad pathways. If you're in India, that's [INBO](/inbo-preparation/). If you're in the US, that's [USABO](/usabo-preparation/). Other countries have their own pathways; the [IBO official site](https://www.ibo-info.org/) links to national bodies.
