## The challenge

Turkmenistan has sent teams to the International Biology Olympiad for years, but results have been difficult. No Turkmen student has brought home a medal in the past two IBO cycles - not for lack of talent or drive, but for a reason that may be familiar to some smaller delegations: **the students have been using the self-studying technique and passing their knowledge down the generations by their own selves without proper guidance over years.**

The technique that Meryem, her partners, and previous IBO competitors from the past two years have been using is something remarkable in its own way: older students who have been through the process pass what they know to younger ones. The team is largely self-taught, built on a culture of peer mentoring where each generation tries to leave the next one a little better prepared than they were.

But self-study has limits. Resources are hard to come by. Structured programmes in English-language biology olympiad preparation are rare, and the ones that exist often carry a price tag that puts them out of reach. Turkmenistan's biology olympiad students have been making do with whatever they can find - scattered PDFs, secondhand textbooks, and advice from alumni who graduated years ago.

## Why BiOGuide

Meryem, one of six students forming the core of Turkmenistan's current preparation group, heard about BiOGuide's one-year preparation programme and saw something that matched what her team needed:

- **It is completely free.** Every resource, every guide, every practice paper - no paywalls, no premium tiers, no "free trial" that expires. For a team operating on limited means in a country where international subscriptions are not straightforward, this matters more than any single feature.

- **It is structured around the IBO syllabus.** BiOGuide's notes are organised into the same subject categories that IBO itself tests - Cell Biology, Animal Anatomy and Physiology, Genetics and Evolution, Plant Biology, Ecology, Ethology, and Biosystematics. Instead of hunting across ten different websites for scattered material on each topic, the team can follow a single pathway from start to finish.

- **The one-year study plan gives them a timeline.** Self-study often fails not because the material is too hard, but because there is no pacing - no external structure saying "you should be here by November." BiOGuide's preparation guides lay out a month-by-month progression that gives the team a realistic schedule to follow, with enough flexibility to adapt to their own pace.

## How the team is using it

The six students are working through BiOGuide's resources together. Their approach:

**Subject notes as the backbone.** The members can fo through the Notes@BiOGuide section and read according to their own needs. Any subject, any relevant part. Name it and it is available. What books can't provide, the notes section does. IBO resources compiled into one singular compressed and designated resource.

**BiOrchive for practice.** BiOGuide's archive of past IBO papers with solutions gives the team something they previously struggled to find: real exam-level questions with worked answers. They are using these for timed practice sessions, simulating actual IBO conditions as closely as they can.

**BiOClash for competitive preparation.** When BiOClash rounds open, the individuals plan to participate and compete - not just for the scores, but for the experience of working under timed pressure on original biology problems. The subject-specific rounds align with the topics they are studying each month, giving them a natural checkpoint.

**Discord for community.** The BiOGuide Discord server connects them to students from dozens of other countries - people preparing for the same competition, facing similar challenges, and willing to share advice. For a team that has been largely isolated in their preparation, this is new and valuable.

## What they hope to achieve

Meryem puts it simply: *"We'd genuinely love to see how far we can take it."*

The goal is an IBO medal - but the ambition goes beyond that. If the programme works, the plan is to grow it: bring more Turkmenistan students into the fold, build a larger and more sustainable preparation pipeline, and break the cycle of each generation starting from scratch.

BiOGuide did not create their determination. What it offers is something they have not had before: a free, structured, syllabus-aligned programme that fits the way they already work - peer-driven, self-directed, and built on the belief that students who want to learn should not be held back by what they cannot afford.
