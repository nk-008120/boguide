BIOCLASH — COMPLETE STRUCTURE OVERVIEW
For internal team-meeting use. Non-technical — focused on what matters to a
SCORER, a TEST-TAKER, and a CREATOR. Written 2026-08-11.

====================================================================
GLOSSARY (a few recurring terms, defined once)
====================================================================

Season         — the full year's competition cycle (e.g. "2026-27 season").
                 One champion is crowned per season.

Round          — one individual timed competition within a season (e.g. the
                 Cell Biology round, or the Full Syllabus Test). Each round
                 is presented as a themed "Case File."

Standardized   — a student's score compared against everyone else who took
score /          the SAME round, rather than a raw percentage. Shows how far
"Z-score"        above or below average a student performed. This is what
                 lets an easy round and a hard round be compared fairly on
                 one season ranking, instead of an easy round's scores
                 looking artificially better.

Non-recoverable — a question that, once answered and confirmed, can never be
lock              revisited or changed. Used specifically for questions
                  where the reveal of the correct answer (or related
                  content) would spoil something else in the paper.

Extension block — an optional +30 minutes a test-taker can request mid-round.
                  Not free — it costs a small, pre-declared amount off that
                  student's standardized score for the round.

====================================================================
1. WHAT BIOCLASH IS
====================================================================

BiOClash is BiOGuide's own original biology competition — not a reproduction
of IBO/USABO papers (that's BiOrchive, a separate part of the site). Every
question is written and reviewed in-house. Rounds are presented with a light
Time Variance Authority / Loki-inspired narrative wrapper, but the actual
academic content stays serious and exam-standard throughout — the theming is
flavor at the edges, never inside the real questions.

====================================================================
2. THE SEASON CALENDAR
====================================================================

A season is made of EIGHT rounds: seven subject-focused rounds spread across
the year, plus one capstone round covering everything.

  Subject round               Season weight
  ---------------------------------------------
  Cell Biology                     20%,Sep
  Animal Anatomy & Physiology      25%,Nov
  Genetics & Evolution             20%,Jan
  Plant Anatomy & Physiology       15%,Mar
  Ecology                          10%,May
  Ethology                          5%,May
  Biosystematics                    5%,May
  ---------------------------------------------
  Full Syllabus Test (FST)        100%   <- the capstone, worth as much as
                                            all seven subject rounds combined

The FST opens roughly 3 weeks before IBO each year, with results announced
about 1 week before IBO — its date moves with IBO's actual date each year,
it isn't fixed to a calendar month.

A student's contribution to the season ranking, from each round, is that
round's standardized score multiplied by that round's weight above. These
add up across every round a student took part in — a round not attempted
simply contributes nothing, it isn't a penalty beyond the missed opportunity.

====================================================================
3. FOR TEST-TAKERS
====================================================================

STARTING A ROUND
  Each round is open for a window before it closes to new starts — 3 days
  for a subject round, a full week for the Full Syllabus round. A student
  can begin any time within that window; once they click Start, their own
  personal timer begins from that moment (not from when the window opened).

TIME LIMITS
  Subject rounds: 90 minutes.
  Full Syllabus Test: 180 minutes (3 hours).
  If more time is needed, a student can request up to three extra 30-minute
  blocks DURING their attempt — but each block costs a small, pre-declared
  amount off that student's standardized ranking for the round, and the
  exact cost is always shown before they confirm. Finishing without needing
  any extra time earns a small bonus, bigger if many other students in that
  same round did need extra time.

ONE CONTINUOUS SITTING
  There is no pause/break feature. Once started, the clock runs until the
  student submits or time runs out. Only one browser tab or device can hold
  a given attempt open at a time — opening it elsewhere locks the original
  one out.

LOCKED, NON-RECOVERABLE QUESTIONS
  Some questions must be answered and locked in permanently before a
  student can continue — there's no going back to change them. These are
  used specifically where the content that gets revealed next would give
  away the correct answer if the student were still allowed to change it.

CONTENT UNLOCKS PROGRESSIVELY
  A student only ever sees what they've actually earned so far — later
  parts of the paper aren't just hidden by styling, they genuinely don't
  exist on the page yet. There's no way to scroll ahead and preview
  upcoming questions.

BEFORE SUBMITTING
  A student must reach the very last page of the round at least once before
  they're allowed to submit manually — meant to stop an accidental early
  submit. The hard time limit still overrides this the moment it expires,
  regardless.

INTEGRITY COMMITMENTS
  Before starting, a student checks off a list of individual commitments
  (not one blanket agreement) — not switching tabs or windows, not taking
  screenshots or recording the screen, waiting a few seconds after major
  button clicks instead of clicking repeatedly, and recording their own
  front camera for the whole attempt and uploading it afterward for
  verification.

HOW RESULTS ARE JUDGED
  A result is NOT a raw percentage. It's standardized against everyone else
  who took that same round (similar to how real olympiads often report
  results), so an easy round and a hard round can be compared fairly on one
  scale. Students should not compare a raw mark total directly against a
  friend's — relative standing within the round is what actually counts.

RESULTS TAKE TIME
  Most marks come from free-response answers, graded by a person, not a
  computer — results are never instant after submitting.

====================================================================
4. FOR SCORERS
====================================================================

WHEN GRADING STARTS
  Once a round's window closes, every submitted (or timed-out) attempt is
  ready to be graded.

GRADING
  Multiple-choice, true/false, and numeric questions can be checked
  mechanically against the answer key. Free-response questions need a
  human judgment call against that round's marking notes. Every correct
  answer's mark value adds up into a student's RAW SCORE for the round.

TURNING RAW SCORES INTO A RANKING
  Raw scores are converted into a STANDARDIZED score relative to everyone
  else who completed that same round — this reflects how far above or
  below the round's own average a student performed, not a raw percentage.
  This is what makes an easy round and a hard round comparable on one
  scale later.

  If a student used any extra-time blocks, that's factored in at this same
  stage — using extra time costs a small, pre-declared amount off their
  standardized score; finishing without needing any earns a small bonus.
  This can only be done once every score in the round is known.

  IMPORTANT: with a small number of test-takers (likely the case for most
  rounds, especially early on), standardized scores can be sensitive to a
  single unusually strong or weak result skewing everyone else's number.
  Always sanity-check the spread of scores before treating them as final —
  don't just run the numbers and publish blindly.

SEASON WEIGHTING
  Each round has a fixed weight toward the season standing (see the
  calendar above). A student's contribution to the season ranking, per
  round, is: that round's standardized score x that round's weight. These
  sum across every round a student attempted that season.

LEADERBOARDS
  Every round gets its own permanent leaderboard once results are entered
  — it never gets overwritten by a later round. Separately, there is ONE
  cumulative season leaderboard, summing every round's weighted
  contribution, which is the most prominent ranking on the site.

STILL TO BUILD
  A cross-submission similarity check for free-response answers (to catch
  copied or collaborated answers) is planned but not built yet — it's
  intentionally deferred until there's a real batch of multi-student
  submissions to build and calibrate it against.

THE MATH, BRIEFLY
  For anyone who wants the actual formulas rather than just the plain-
  English description above. Nothing here is code — just arithmetic a
  scorer could work through by hand or in a spreadsheet.

  Step 1 — Raw score.
    raw_i = student i's total marks earned in round R. No formula, just
    the sum of every component's marks that was graded correct.

  Step 2 — Standardize into a Z-score, using the WHOLE field that
  completed round R (n students):

    mean_R  = (raw_1 + raw_2 + ... + raw_n) / n

    stdev_R = square root of [ ((raw_1 - mean_R)^2 + (raw_2 - mean_R)^2
              + ... + (raw_n - mean_R)^2) / n ]

    z_i     = (raw_i - mean_R) / stdev_R

    By construction, the Z-scores for any round always average to 0, with
    a spread of 1 — that's what makes a Z-score from an easy round
    directly comparable to a Z-score from a hard round, even though the
    raw marks behind them mean completely different things.

  Step 3 — Adjust for extension blocks used, to get the TIME-NORMALIZED
  Z-score (the number that actually gets used everywhere else):

    Let q = the fraction of round R's field that used ONE OR MORE
    extension blocks (e.g. if 3 of 10 students used at least one block,
    q = 0.3).

    cost(blocks used)  = the round's pre-declared marginal cost, summed
                          up through however many blocks that student
                          used (e.g. a schedule of 0.10, then +0.20, then
                          +0.35 means using all three blocks costs
                          0.10 + 0.20 + 0.35 = 0.65 total)

    bonus(blocks used) = a small fixed bonus amount x q, but ONLY for a
                          student who used zero blocks — everyone else
                          gets no bonus

    time_normalized_z_i = z_i - cost(blocks used) + bonus(blocks used)

  Step 4 — Roll every round a student attempted into one season number:

    season_index = (round 1's weight x round 1's time-normalized Z)
                  + (round 2's weight x round 2's time-normalized Z)
                  + ... for every round attempted that season

    A round never attempted simply isn't in the sum — no penalty term,
    it just contributes zero.

  A worked example (4 students, one round, illustrative numbers only):

    Student | Raw  | Blocks used | Z      | Cost  | Bonus | Time-norm. Z
    --------|------|--------------|--------|-------|-------|-------------
    Alice   | 140  | 0            | +1.28  | 0     | +0.11 | +1.40
    Carol   | 125  | 1            | +0.47  | -0.10 | 0     | +0.37
    Bob     | 110  | 2            | -0.34  | -0.30 | 0     | -0.64
    Dan     |  90  | 3            | -1.42  | -0.65 | 0     | -2.07

    (Round mean here was 116.25, standard deviation 18.50, and 3 of the
    4 students used at least one extension block, so q = 0.75 — that's
    why Alice's zero-block bonus is fairly generous here: most of the
    field needed extra time and she didn't.)

  A note on small classes: for any round with n students, no single
  Z-score can mathematically go beyond the square root of (n - 1). With
  4 students that ceiling is about 1.73; with 10 it's 3; with 30 it's
  about 5.4. Small rounds will often look "noisier" than this — one
  unusually strong or weak result can swing everyone else's number more
  than it would in a larger field. This is exactly why the "sanity-check
  before publishing" note above matters.

====================================================================
5. FOR CREATORS
====================================================================

ROUND STRUCTURE
  Each round is built as a "Case File" — a themed paper made of several
  parts, each part covering a chunk of that round's subject scope.

RECOVERABLE VS. NON-RECOVERABLE PARTS
  A part can either stay freely revisable until final submission, or
  contain "non-recoverable" questions that lock permanently the moment
  they're answered. Use non-recoverable locks specifically where the next
  reveal would spoil the correct answer if a student could still go back
  and change it — not as a default for every question.

QUESTION TYPES AVAILABLE
  Multiple choice, true/false, numeric, free-response, plain
  informational/context blocks (not questions, just required reading), and
  one specialised type that dynamically asks "why is each of the OTHER
  options wrong" based on whichever option the student actually picked —
  useful specifically so there's never a predictable, un-asked-about
  leftover option that gives the answer away by elimination.

NOTHING IS VISIBLE BEFORE IT'S EARNED
  A student never sees content before they've unlocked it — not hidden by
  styling, genuinely absent until earned. When writing a round, be
  deliberate about what a LATER part's setup or narrative text reveals —
  wording that reads naturally can accidentally spoil an earlier
  non-recoverable answer if it isn't checked carefully against everything
  that comes before it.

MARKS AND DURATION SHOULD MATCH
  Every question carries an explicit mark value, and a round has a total
  mark count. A round's time limit (90 minutes for a subject round, 180
  for the Full Syllabus round, plus the extension mechanic on top) should
  be scoped to roughly match how much content and how many marks the round
  actually contains — not simply copied from a different round without
  checking the fit. (Flagging this because the very first round built,
  Cell Biology, has more content than its current 90-minute slot
  comfortably fits — a known, unresolved sizing mismatch worth discussing,
  not something to silently copy into future rounds.)

WHO CAN ATTEMPT A ROUND
  This has changed: rounds used to be restricted to one specific invited
  account for internal testing. Now, once a round is live, ANY logged-in
  site user can attempt it — going live is a real, public event now, not a
  private test.

ANSWER KEYS AND MARKING NOTES
  Reference material used for grading a round must be kept somewhere a
  student can never reach — never shipped to a student's device at any
  point, even in a hidden or stripped-down form.

====================================================================
6. INTEGRITY & FAIR PLAY (shared context for everyone)
====================================================================

TWO HONEST CATEGORIES
  Everything protecting the competition's integrity falls into one of two
  categories, and the team should always know which is which internally,
  even where students aren't told the difference:
    - REAL, technical enforcement — e.g. content genuinely never being
      sent to a student before it's earned, or a locked answer genuinely
      being unchangeable afterward.
    - AGREED-TO RULES with logging for review — e.g. the video-recording
      requirement, the no-screenshot rule, or not closing the browser tab.
      These rely on the student's honesty; a website cannot fully enforce
      them. They still have real value (they raise the effort/risk of
      casual cheating and create a record if a case needs review), but
      they are not the same kind of guarantee as the first category.

BIGGEST OPEN RISK
  Nothing currently technically confirms that the person taking a round is
  actually the enrolled student — this is a known, accepted trust-based
  risk while the platform is still small. Worth revisiting if it ever
  becomes a real, demonstrated problem, not something to solve
  preemptively right now.

NOTHING IS AUTO-PENALIZED
  Every "soft" signal collected during an attempt (switching tabs,
  losing the window's focus, closing the tab) is logged purely for a
  human to optionally review later — none of it automatically changes a
  student's score or standing.

====================================================================
7. RECOGNITION
====================================================================

  - Every round crowns its own top finishers and keeps that record
    permanently, separate from every other round.
  - Across the whole season, the cumulative standardized ranking crowns
    one overall season champion — the single most prominent ranking shown
    on the site.
  - Top season finishers may be recognized with an honorary position on
    the BiOGuide team.
