Cancer immunotherapy is a cancer treatment approach where the body’s immune cells attack tumor
cells. During their development, tumor cells accumulate many neoantigens (new antigens). The more
neoantigens present on the cell surface of tumor cells, the more efficiently immune cells destroy them.
'Immune checkpoint' is a term that defines the primary interaction between an immune cell and a tumor
cell, and in which the immune cell 'decides' whether to attack the tumor cell or not.
However, tumor cells can avoid immune surveillance by hijacking the physiological pathway controlling
T-cell activity (Figure 1A). To prevent the escape of tumor cells from immune attack, checkpoint inhibitors
have been developed (Figure 1A).
