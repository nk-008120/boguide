DNA fiber assay is used to study various aspects of DNA synthesis, such as replication speed, replication
origin firing, and replication termination. It utilizes modified nucleotides to visualize the progression
of DNA replication directly. In this assay, cellular DNA is sequentially labeled with chloro-deoxyuridine
(CldU) followed by iodo-deoxyuridine (IdU), each for an equal period. Upon replication, cells incorporate
these modified nucleotides into newly synthesized DNA. Next, cells are lysed and individual DNA fibers
are stretched on a microscopic slide, followed by CldU (red) and IdU (blue) antibody staining. Finally,
DNA fibers are visualized by fluorescent microscopy.
Figure 1. Panels A-F represent replicated DNA segments. Each panel shows a single unin-
terrupted DNA molecule labeled with CldU (red) followed by IdU (blue). The white bar at the
bottom right of each panel is a scale bar of 10 μm. The DNA in all panels is from the same
organism.
On your answer sheet, indicate "T" for true statements and "F" for false
ones.

while panel B shows replication progression from two forks.

C. In panel B, CldU is incorporated into either a leading or lagging strand of DNA
but not into both.
 
D. The DNA belongs to a bacterial species.
