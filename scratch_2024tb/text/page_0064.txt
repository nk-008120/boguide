In the liver lobule, 75% of the blood comes via the portal vein located within the portal triad and 25%
comes from the hepatic artery. The portal triad consists of a hepatic artery, a portal vein, and a bile duct.
The portal vein carries blood from the intestine and stomach, while the central veins carry blood from
the liver cells. Functionally, in the lobule, there are zones: 1, 2, 3 (Figure 1a). Figure 1c shows bile flow in
different animals.
Figure 1. A S – portal triad; Q – central vein; 1 – periportal zone; 2 – middle zone; 3 – perivenous
zone. B Y – circulating toxic substance concentration (%); X – distance from the central vein. C
Y – Bile flow (µL/kg/min); X – Bile salt secretion (µmol/kg/min); A – Rabbit; B – Rat; C – Human.
"kg" in units refers to kg of liver.
