Dichotomous keys are essential tools in taxonomy. They consist of a series of statements, each highlight-
ing a distinct morphological characteristic that may be true or false depending on the organism under
observation. As the organism is compared with these statements, the key systematically directs the user
through a decision tree, ultimately leading to a specific taxonomic classification (Figure 1).
Figure 1. Dichotomous tree model with three levels; T - true, F - false.
To determine the species of an unknown organism, referred to as X, you utilize a hypothetical dichoto-
mous key with 10 levels, structurally similar to the one depicted in Figure 1. The final level of this tree
provides a conclusive taxonomic identification, with no further branching points. The tree has a complete
set of nodes without terminating branches before the final level.
On your answer sheet, indicate "T" for true statements and "F" for false
ones.
 
A. Determining the species of an organism with more complex morphological
structures than that of organism X using the provided key will take more than
9 steps.
 
B. The maximal number of species that could be identified with the key is 512.
 
C. An identification step at level 3 (counting from the top) has four times more
possible final taxonomic outcomes than an identification step at level 5.
 
D. Consider three taxa A, B, and C. If A and B share a more recent common
ancestor than A and C, then the paths along the dichotomous key leading to A
and B will certainly diverge at an earlier step than the paths leading to A and C.
