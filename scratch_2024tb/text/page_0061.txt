Pharmacokinetics studies drug movement in the body, guiding dosage for desired effects based on bio-
chemical properties. Table 1 shows pharmacokinetic properties that regulate the duration of a drug's
therapeutic effect.
Property
Description
Absorption
Influenced by ionization and other biochemical properties of the com-
pound
Distribution
Influenced by polarity, transport protein binding, and blood flow. The
volume of distribution (V) describes a drug's ability to disperse within
body fluids. A V close to the total body volume indicates the best distri-
bution.
Metabolism
Primarily occurs in the liver. First-pass metabolism happens when the
liver metabolizes a drug before it enters systemic circulation. The drug's
half-life is the time for its concentration in the blood to halve. A prodrug
is a drug that becomes active after being metabolized (typically in the
liver).
Excretion
Kidneys typically eliminate hydrophilic substances, while hydrophobic
ones exit via bile. Only 5% of bile salts (as well as drugs) are excreted,
with the remainder reabsorbed, transported to the liver, and secreted
again with bile. This is known as enterohepatic circulation.
