Genetics

A Molecular Approach — Advanced Undergraduate Series

Inspired by the structure and depth of iGenetics: A Molecular Approach by Robert J. Brooker

Overview

Genetics is the scientific study of heredity, variation, and the molecular mechanisms by which information encoded in DNA is expressed, transmitted, and evolved across generations. This section bridges classical Mendelian principles with cutting-edge genomics, providing a rigorous foundation for advanced undergraduate study.

Each unit is organized to build conceptually — from the transmission of traits, through the molecular machinery of the gene, toward the population-level and biotechnological implications of genetic variation.

Unit I — Mendelian & Extensions of Mendelian Genetics

Laying the quantitative and conceptual foundation.

Chapter Title Key Concepts
1.1 Mendel’s Laws of Inheritance Segregation, Independent Assortment, monohybrid & dihybrid crosses
1.2 Extensions of Mendelism Incomplete dominance, codominance, multiple alleles, ABO blood groups
1.3 Epistasis & Gene Interaction Recessive/dominant epistasis, complementation, novel phenotypic ratios
1.4 Quantitative Genetics Polygenic inheritance, heritability, norm of reaction
1.5 Probability & Statistical Analysis Product rule, sum rule, binomial distribution, chi-square test

Interactive Tools:

  • 🧬 Punnett Square Simulator — mono/dihybrid, codominance & incomplete dominance modes
  • 🌳 Pedigree Chart Builder — autosomal/X-linked, dominant/recessive pattern inference

Unit II — Chromosomal Theory & Variation

From abstract genes to physical chromosomes.

Chapter Title Key Concepts
2.1 Chromosomal Theory of Inheritance Sutton-Boveri hypothesis, mitosis vs. meiosis review
2.2 Sex Determination & Sex-Linked Traits X-linkage, dosage compensation, Lyon hypothesis, Y-linked traits
2.3 Genetic Linkage & Mapping Recombination frequency, three-point crosses, interference & coincidence
2.4 Variation in Chromosome Number Aneuploidy (monosomy, trisomy), polyploidy, autopolyploidy vs. allopolyploidy
2.5 Variation in Chromosome Structure Deletions, duplications, inversions, translocations — mechanisms & consequences

Interactive Tools:

  • 🔬 Karyotype Viewer — human karyograms with trisomy, monosomy, and translocation cases

Unit III — Molecular Genetics: DNA Structure & Replication

The gene as a physical molecule.

Chapter Title Key Concepts
3.1 Molecular Structure of DNA & RNA Watson-Crick double helix, base pairing, antiparallel strands, RNA types
3.2 DNA Replication Semiconservative model, origins of replication, leading/lagging strand synthesis
3.3 Replication Machinery DNA polymerase fidelity, primase, helicase, ligase, SSBPs, clamp loader
3.4 DNA Repair Mismatch repair, nucleotide excision repair, base excision repair, double-strand break repair
3.5 Telomeres & Telomerase End-replication problem, telomerase mechanism, implications in aging & cancer

Interactive Tools:

  • 🎞️ DNA Replication Animation — step-through visualization of leading/lagging strand synthesis at the replication fork

Unit IV — Gene Expression & Regulation

From sequence to function — transcription, translation, and control.

Chapter Title Key Concepts
4.1 Transcription in Prokaryotes RNA polymerase, promoter recognition (σ factor), elongation, termination
4.2 Transcription in Eukaryotes RNA Pol I/II/III, general transcription factors, enhancers, mediator complex
4.3 RNA Processing 5′ capping, 3′ polyadenylation, splicing (spliceosome), alternative splicing
4.4 Translation Genetic code, tRNA charging, ribosome structure, initiation-elongation-termination
4.5 Regulation in Prokaryotes Operons (lac, trp), repressors, activators, attenuation, riboswitches
4.6 Regulation in Eukaryotes Chromatin remodeling, histone modification, DNA methylation, transcription factors
4.7 Post-transcriptional Regulation miRNA, siRNA, RNAi pathway, mRNA stability

Interactive Tools:

  • 🎞️ Transcription & Translation Animation — coupled mRNA synthesis, processing, and ribosomal translation

Unit V — Population & Evolutionary Genetics

Genes in populations — allele frequencies, selection, and speciation.

Chapter Title Key Concepts
5.1 Hardy-Weinberg Equilibrium Allele/genotype frequency calculation, assumptions, deviations as evolutionary signals
5.2 Natural Selection Directional, stabilizing, disruptive selection; fitness and selection coefficients
5.3 Genetic Drift Bottleneck effect, founder effect, effective population size
5.4 Mutation & Migration Mutation-selection balance, gene flow, FST
5.5 Non-random Mating Inbreeding coefficient, inbreeding depression, assortative mating
5.6 Molecular Evolution Neutral theory, molecular clocks, synonymous vs. nonsynonymous substitutions

Interactive Tools:

  • 📊 Hardy-Weinberg Calculator — allele frequency inputs, multi-generation simulation with drift & selection overlays

Unit VI — Genomics & Biotechnology

Manipulating, sequencing, and interpreting genomes.

Chapter Title Key Concepts
6.1 Recombinant DNA Technology Restriction enzymes, cloning vectors, ligation, transformation, library construction
6.2 PCR & Its Applications Standard PCR, RT-PCR, qPCR, allele-specific PCR
6.3 DNA Sequencing Sanger sequencing, Next-Generation Sequencing (NGS), long-read platforms
6.4 Genomics & Bioinformatics Whole-genome sequencing, annotation, BLAST, comparative genomics
6.5 Transcriptomics & Proteomics RNA-seq, microarrays, mass spectrometry, proteome mapping
6.6 CRISPR-Cas9 & Genome Editing Cas9 mechanism, guide RNA design, HDR vs. NHEJ, therapeutic applications
6.7 Ethical, Legal & Social Implications GMOs, gene therapy, privacy in genomics, germline editing debates
